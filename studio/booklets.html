.Studio · folded-card booklet format · v0.1

A 100-pack of cards is also 4 mini notebooks

Fold a 4×6 card in half along its short axis. You get a 4×3 booklet page — landscape orientation, comfortable thumb-and-finger grip, fits a pocket. Seven cards stapled together = a 28-page mini notebook. Thirteen cards = a 52-page standard booklet. A 100-pack of cards transforms into 4 mini notebooks OR 2 standard booklets. Same cards. New use.

The fold geometry

Why 4×3 landscape

A 4×6 card folds two ways. Short-side fold gives a 4×3 booklet page (landscape orientation, near-Field-Notes proportion). Long-side fold gives a 2×6 booklet page (awkwardly tall, hard to write on). The short-side fold is the natural choice and the studio's standard.

One card = 4 page faces when folded. Several cards nested into each other and stapled = a booklet. The studio's standard binding is two staples on the fold line, ~1" from each end.

The capacity math

What a 100-pack actually makes

Cards per booklet × number of booklets, exact remainders matter. A 100-pack of Oxford 4×6 cards yields different booklet configurations depending on the format. The two clean unit-divisions — 4 mini notebooks of 25 pages each, or 2 standard notebooks of 50 pages each — are the .Studio canonical formats.

Text capacity per booklet

What can be printed

For studio-printed manuals and other content, the typography spec matches the rest of the .Studio register — 9pt EB Garamond body, 11pt leading, 0.25" margins on the 4×3 page. This yields ~118 words per page face.

Custom typesetting · commission service

Beyond the studio's own catalogue, .Studio offers custom booklet typesetting as a commission service. Customer brings text (their own writing, public-domain work, family records); .Studio typesets it to the booklet format and prints a small run. Pricing $80-200 per booklet depending on length and quantity. This is a Year-2 service offering — Year 1 focuses on the catalogue's own SKUs.

Why this matters

A transformation, not a new product

The booklet line uses cards the customer already has. A customer with a 100-pack of 1646 cards can immediately make 4 mini notebooks. .Studio doesn't need to source different paper or invest in new inventory — the booklet is a transformation of an existing product. This is the cleanest possible product line: zero new inventory, infinite combinatorial possibility, every cell-buyer is also a potential booklet-buyer.

The booklet also opens a new content surface for the studio. Editorial work too long for a single card and too short for a full CODEX — the 5,000-word manual range — now has a natural home. The KEEP 1646 mods case study, the Hako owner's manual, the year-end almanac all become real physical products instead of just web pages.

And finally: the booklet compounds with everything else. A MEAL kit can ship with a booklet insert. A CODEX product can have a companion booklet. A BXBX Hako delivery includes its booklet manual. The booklet is a unifying format that connects practices that previously didn't have a shared deliverable.

Production tooling The booklet line needs a small print/fold/staple workflow at studio scale. Print on the customer's existing Pixma laser or a small commercial run; fold by hand or with a bone folder; bind with a long-arm stapler ($30-50 at any office-supply source). Year 1 production is studio-by-hand; if volume warrants, Year 2 can introduce a small saddle-stitch booklet press. Until then, the workflow is intentionally artisanal — each booklet is staple-bound by hand and signed off on by the studio.
