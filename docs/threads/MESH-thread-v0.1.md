## § 0 · What MESH is

**MESH is the studio's build practice for digital products and integration infrastructure.** Where BXBX builds dwellings (physical structures), MESH builds tools, platforms, and integrations (digital systems). The two practices are siblings in the studio's catalogue architecture — both produce buildable artifacts, just in different mediums.

The studio has been doing MESH work for years without naming it. PR0J.co (the production operating system with its 12+ integrated subsystems) is years of unnamed MESH work. CHANNEL (the broadcast-station-in-a-box SaaS concept) is in-progress MESH work. MR-KT.com (the curated creative directory) and EasyVideo.co (the SMB-tier video tool) are existing MESH products. The publishing infrastructure underneath stock.m1nd.co, t1ny.m1nd.co, and the planned mhyc.m1nd.co is operationally MESH. **Naming the practice formalizes what was already happening.**

Going forward, MESH is also where the MHYC matching tool gets built — operationalizing MHYC's neighborhood research into a working interactive product. Same architecture as T1NY:BXBX where T1NY produces research and BXBX produces dwellings.

### Why "MESH"

The name does several jobs:

- **The network metaphor** — a mesh network is a peer-to-peer system where distributed nodes interconnect without a central hub. The studio's digital products are increasingly mesh-shaped: distinct surfaces (stock.m1nd.co, t1ny.m1nd.co, mhyc.m1nd.co, PR0J.co) that interconnect rather than feeding into one monolithic platform. **MESH is the architecture, not just the brand.**
- **The fabric metaphor** — mesh is woven, flexible, takes the shape of what it covers. Digital infrastructure should serve the studio's editorial and product practices, not impose a new shape on them. *Fabric, not architecture* is the register.
- **The visual register** — four letters, all-caps, sits typographically alongside BXBX, KEEP, COOK, STOCK, BDRC, GROW, SPORE. Doesn't need number-letter substitution (M1ND, T1NY, B1RN, MHYC's siblings use that idiom; MESH is a real word doing real work). The studio's naming holds heterogeneously by design.

## § 1 · The catalogue architecture, finally legible

MESH completes the studio's catalogue architecture and makes it readable as a 2×2:

| | Research practice | Build practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical / dwelling-scale** | T1NY | BXBX |
| **Geographic / network-scale** | MHYC | MESH |

Each row is a paired practice: research operates at one scale, build practice operates at the same scale producing buildable outputs from the research. Each column is a function: research practices publish editorial/analytical artifacts; build practices produce shippable products.

Beyond the 2×2:

- **STOCK + COOK + BDRC + GROW + SPORE** form an elaborated build-practice family for food and domestic production. No separate research-practice umbrella exists yet; MEAL.0001 was a one-off consulting research artifact, not a continuous practice. If the food research expands enough to warrant continuity, it could become its own research practice over time (working name candidate: **FOOD** or **MEAL**).
- **KEEP** is a quieter build-practice that operates as supporting infrastructure — providing the storage architecture (1646 cells, Wowlive shelving) for the other practices.

The catalogue now reads as **two paired practices, one elaborated build-family, one supporting-infrastructure practice.** That's coherent architecture. The kind of structural integrity that's usually deliberate; here, it emerged through iteration and only became visible when MESH got named.

### The pairing principle

Each research/build pair operates on the same underlying questions but produces different artifact types:

- **T1NY** asks how dwellings work; **BXBX** builds specific dwellings
- **MHYC** asks how neighborhoods relate; **MESH** builds the digital tools that operationalize the relationships

A reader of either research practice can find the corresponding build practice when they want to act on what they've read. A customer of either build practice can find the research practice when they want to understand the broader context. **The studio's catalogue serves curiosity and action equally.**

## § 2 · What MESH produces · the four product categories

MESH builds in four distinct categories. Each has its own design pattern, its own engineering register, and its own relationship to the rest of the studio.

### 2.1 · Studio publishing infrastructure

The web surfaces underneath the studio's editorial practices: m1nd.co, stock.m1nd.co, t1ny.m1nd.co, mhyc.m1nd.co, and any future sibling surfaces. The Ghost-or-equivalent installations, the editorial design systems, the email delivery, the archive search, the cross-publication navigation.

This category is **the unglamorous backbone** but it's the foundation everything else stands on. Most of the work here is sensible defaults, careful selection of tools (Ghost over WordPress, for example), and a shared design system that makes seven distinct publishing surfaces feel like one coherent studio.

Existing in this category: m1nd.co itself, the planned stock.m1nd.co launch infrastructure.
Pending in this category: t1ny.m1nd.co and mhyc.m1nd.co stand-up.

### 2.2 · Interactive tools and products

The things customers come to MESH for directly. The MHYC matching tool is the canonical example: a graphical interactive surface where a user picks a neighborhood and gets curated analogues. The MHYC matching tool is the inaugural Phase 0 product in this category and gets its own engineering scope (§ 3 below).

Existing in this category: MR-KT.com (curated creative directory), EasyVideo.co (SMB video tool).
Pending in this category: MHYC matching tool, possible CHANNEL (broadcast-station-in-a-box).

### 2.3 · Internal studio operations infrastructure

The systems the studio uses to operate. PR0J.co is the canonical example — a production OS with 12+ integrated subsystems that the studio has been using and refining for years. Order management for STOCK fulfillment will eventually live here. Customer relationship management. Inventory tracking for 1646 cells, grain stocks, mushroom spawn, GROW seed inventory. Studio email and document infrastructure.

This category is **internally-facing** and may never have customer-visible surfaces. Its products serve the studio's own operations. Worth naming MESH-internal so the studio doesn't accidentally treat operational infrastructure as customer products or vice versa.

Existing in this category: PR0J.co.
Pending in this category: STOCK order management system, possible customer CRM if the studio scales beyond direct relationships.

### 2.4 · Studio-wide integrations

The connective tissue between practices, surfaces, and customer touchpoints. A customer who buys a Hako from BXBX should be able to find their relevant T1NY essays, their MHYC neighborhood profile (wherever they site the Hako), their BDRC grain catalogue, their KEEP archive cells, all under one studio account. A customer who reads a MHYC neighborhood profile should be able to easily discover that the studio also designs BXBX dwellings that suit that neighborhood's typology.

This is where MESH earns its name most directly. **The integration layer is mesh-shaped:** distributed studio practices connected via shared identity, shared search, shared design language, shared catalogue navigation. Not a monolithic super-platform but a network of distinct surfaces that recognize each other.

Existing in this category: nothing yet, formally. Informal cross-linking between m1nd.co properties exists.
Pending in this category: unified studio account / identity layer, cross-practice search, the cross-reference card system inside cells (BDRC Cell 05 references GROW Cell 01 etc. — *that pattern is itself a MESH product even though it lives on paper cards*).

## § 3 · MHYC matching tool · the inaugural Phase 0 product

The MHYC thread (v0.1) sketched the matching tool's architecture in detail. That sketch now belongs here as MESH's inaugural Phase 0 product. Brief recap and refinement:

### Product summary

At **mhyc.m1nd.co/match**: a graphical map-based UI where a user selects an anchor neighborhood and the tool returns 3-7 curated analogues with editorial commentary explaining each match. Match quality categorized as **Strong / Adjacent / Aspirational**. AI-suggested candidates, editor-curated final matches.

### Technical architecture (v0.1)

```
User picks anchor neighborhood on map
       ↓
Vector embedding lookup against studio neighborhood-profile corpus
       ↓  
Top ~20 candidate matches by embedding similarity
       ↓
Editorial filter (Jordan or contributors): confirm / reject / note as adjacent
       ↓
Published match set with commentary
       ↓
User sees curated matches with map + editorial copy
```

### Build stack proposal

- **Frontend:** Svelte (lightweight, good for static-site interactivity) or Astro (if SEO matters more)
- **Maps:** Mapbox (industry standard, good quality, paid tier needed once traffic scales but free tier works for Phase 0)
- **Backend:** static-first with optional Supabase or similar for any dynamic functionality
- **Embedding generation:** OpenAI text-embedding-3-large or BGE-large, run once per profile and cached
- **Editorial CMS:** same Ghost instance as mhyc.m1nd.co publishes the underlying neighborhood profiles; the matching tool reads from Ghost's API and overlays its own match-relationship data
- **Hosting:** Cloudflare Pages or Vercel for the matching tool itself

### Engineering scope estimate

For a competent web developer with Mapbox experience, the v0.1 matching tool is roughly a **2-3 week sprint** including design polish. Major work items:

1. Mapbox base layer setup with neighborhood polygons (Week 1)
2. Anchor-search interaction + embedding-based candidate lookup (Week 1-2)
3. Match display UI with commentary + tier labels (Week 2)
4. Editorial CMS integration to pull neighborhood profiles (Week 2)
5. Polish, mobile responsive, accessibility (Week 3)

Phase 0 ships with the first ~8 neighborhood profiles in the corpus (per MHYC-thread v0.2). The tool grows in usefulness as the corpus grows.

### Why this is a strong inaugural MESH product

It demonstrates the practice in one product:

- **Integration:** the tool integrates Ghost editorial content with custom interactive UI (Category 2.2 product depending on Category 2.1 infrastructure)
- **Studio character:** the AI-assisted-but-editor-curated approach is the studio's editorial register made manifest in a digital product (algorithm proposes, judgment disposes)
- **Catalogue interaction:** the matching tool benefits MHYC (drives traffic to the editorial corpus) AND potentially benefits BXBX (a customer matched to a Lisbon neighborhood becomes a candidate for a portable BXBX dwelling sited there) AND potentially benefits T1NY (the matched neighborhoods become subjects for future T1NY case studies)
- **Defensibility:** the studio's editorial corpus is the hard-to-replicate asset. The matching tool is just the surface that makes the corpus usable — and the corpus is what gets harder to compete with as it grows.

## § 5 · MESH's relationship to AI / agent systems

Worth flagging directly because this is where MESH gets interesting in 2026 specifically.

The studio operates in a moment where individual MESH products that would have taken months of work in 2018 can be assembled in days using AI-assisted development, vibe coding, and modern integration tools. The matching tool described in § 3 is achievable in 2-3 weeks specifically because the AI tooling has matured.

This means MESH can punch above its weight. A studio with thoughtful product design judgment and AI-assisted execution can ship more digital products than the same studio could have a few years ago. **MESH's editorial role — deciding what's worth building, what register it should have, what the studio's products should NOT be — becomes proportionally more important** as the cost of building drops.

This is a place where the studio's broader editorial register (visible across STOCK's hanko system, BXBX's restrained design language, T1NY's research-grade publishing) becomes a real competitive advantage at MESH-scale. **Most AI-assisted-built digital products have terrible design taste.** The studio doesn't. MESH products will be visibly different from the deluge of vibe-coded SaaS being produced in 2026 — and that difference is defensible.

### Implications for how MESH operates

- **MESH does not build everything in-house.** Some MESH products are built by the studio directly; others are commissioned to outside developers under studio direction. The studio's role in both cases is **product judgment + design direction + editorial taste**, not necessarily the engineering itself.
- **MESH benefits from a coherent design system across products.** Same typography, same restraint, same chrome language as STOCK and BXBX. A user encountering a MESH product recognizes it as part of the studio without being told.
- **MESH products are deliberately small.** Resist the temptation to build comprehensive platforms; build focused tools that do one thing well, integrate where useful, replace if better tools emerge. Same architectural humility as the studio's commodity-envelopes thesis.

## § 6 · Operating questions worth flagging

**Q1 · Naming convention for MESH products.** Studio products use `PRACTICE-NNN` (hyphenated, 3-digit) — BXBX-005, BDRC Cell 09, GROW Cell 01. **Should MESH products follow the same pattern (MESH-001 = PR0J.co, MESH-002 = MR-KT.com etc.)?** My read: yes, internally. Customer-facing the products retain their own names (PR0J.co stays PR0J.co publicly). Internal documentation uses MESH-NNN for cross-referencing.

**Q2 · The PR0J.co identity question.** PR0J.co has its own brand, its own URL, its own customer relationships. **Does it stay an independent product surface that happens to be a MESH practice product, or does it get visually absorbed into the studio's MESH catalogue?** My read: independent surface, MESH-documented internally. PR0J.co keeps its existing identity.

**Q3 · The customer-facing presence of MESH.** Does MESH have a public landing page at **mesh.m1nd.co** the way T1NY and MHYC do? My read: yes eventually but **lower priority than T1NY and MHYC.** The research practices benefit most from public-facing surfaces (they ARE editorial publication); MESH's products have their own surfaces (PR0J.co, MR-KT.com, etc.), so MESH-the-practice page is mostly studio-credibility-positioning. Worth a small landing page documenting the practice and listing the products. Phase 1 or 2.

**Q4 · Build capacity for MESH at the studio.** The studio has Jordan's design and editorial judgment, plus AI-assisted development capability. **Is that sufficient for the MESH product pipeline, or does MESH eventually need dedicated development hires?** My read: AI-assisted + occasional contract developers for ~2 years. Once MESH has 3-4 active customer-facing products under maintenance, a part-time dedicated developer becomes worth considering. Until then, scope MESH ambitiously but execute lean.

**Q5 · Maintenance and sunsetting.** Digital products have ongoing maintenance costs (security patches, framework upgrades, customer support). **What's the studio's policy for sunsetting MESH products that no longer pay their maintenance cost?** My read: honest assessment annually. A MESH product that costs more in maintenance than it returns in value to the studio gets archived, with a clear customer-facing note and any data export the customer needs. Don't keep zombie products alive out of inertia.

**Q6 · Integration across practices · scope and priority.** Section 2.4 sketched studio-wide integrations as a MESH category. **Which integrations matter most for Phase 0-1?** My read:
- **Highest priority:** shared studio account / identity layer (a customer who buys a Hako, an iDOO seed cell, and an MHYC subscription should manage all three from one place eventually)
- **High priority:** cross-practice navigation (a STOCK customer landing on stock.m1nd.co should find their way to MHYC neighborhood profiles or T1NY tiny dwelling research naturally if they're interested)
- **Lower priority:** unified search across all studio surfaces (nice-to-have, not Phase 0)

**Q7 · Open-source posture.** Many MESH products could plausibly open-source their code (the matching tool's frontend, for example). **What's the studio's posture on open-sourcing MESH work?** My read: open-source liberally where the studio's value-add isn't the code itself. The matching tool's *frontend code* is generic; the *editorial corpus* of confirmed matches is the studio's actual asset and stays proprietary. **Open-source the bones, keep the editorial layer.**
