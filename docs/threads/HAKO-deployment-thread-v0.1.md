## § 0 · What this document is

The studio has spent May 2026 producing thread specs for five practices: BXBX (dwellings), KEEP (archives), STOCK (cuisine refills) with sub-lines COOK + BDRC, GROW (hydroponics), and SPORE (mushroom cultivation). Each thread answers its own questions independently. **This document is the first thread that requires all five to operate together** — inside a single 37 sq ft dwelling envelope that fits in a U-Haul U-Box for transit.

The Hako-deployment-thread is the studio's most ambitious internally-consistent product. It is also, by deliberate design, **the smallest physical dwelling the studio is willing to produce.** Smaller would compromise either the microsystem (no room for the iDOO + rice cooker + SPORE + cell library) or basic livability (sleep, work, hygiene). The U-Box envelope is the smallest envelope that contains the full M1ND.studio thesis.

This is also the first BXBX product the studio has spec'd to production-ready depth. Prior BXBX work (per memory notes: BXBX-005 as flagship, BXBX-016 greenhouse 4×6, BXBX-017 Palram 6×8) established the commodity-envelopes thesis at varying scales. **BXBX-005 is the apex — the smallest dwelling that demonstrates the full studio system.**

## § 1 · The envelope · what we're working with

### 1.1 The U-Box dimensional reality

Per U-Haul published specifications (confirmed 2026):

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Interior length | 95" (2.41 m) |
| Interior width | 56" (1.42 m) |
| Interior height | 83.5" (2.12 m) |
| Floor area | 36.9 sq ft (3.43 m²) |
| Interior volume | 257 cu ft (7.28 m³) |
| Maximum loaded weight | 2,000 lb (907 kg) |
| Ground clearance | 6" |

These are *hard* numbers. The Hako cannot exceed them. Anything that doesn't fit in the U-Box envelope doesn't ship — which means the customer's Hako stays where it was built. **The U-Box is both the manufacturing constraint and the shipping vehicle.**

For perspective: 36.9 sq ft is roughly the size of a generous walk-in closet, or about half a typical parking space. **The Hako is a single very-small room.** It is genuinely tiny. Anyone walking inside one experiences it as something between a sleeper-train cabin and a yacht interior.

### 1.2 The headroom math

83.5" interior height is the absolute. Subtract:
- ~3" floor finish (vinyl plank or similar over insulation): leaves 80.5"
- ~3" ceiling finish + insulation + electrical: leaves 77.5"
- That's **6'5" clear headroom** — sufficient for any adult under 6'5" to stand fully upright

For most occupants this is comfortable. For the small portion of customers over 6'5", the Hako will require either a slight slouch at full stance or designed lower-floor sub-zones to accommodate height. **The catalogue should be honest about this** — the Hako fits 99% of the population, not 100%.

### 1.3 The weight discipline

2,000 lb is genuinely tight when everything is included. Rough budget:

| Component | Est. Weight |
|---|---|
| Hako shell structure (framing, insulation, finishes) | 600 lb |
| Built-in millwork (galley wall, KEEP wall, Murphy bed cabinet) | 200 lb |
| Mattress + bedding | 80 lb |
| Microsystem appliances (iDOO + RC503 + SPORE setup + 800W inverter) | 50 lb |
| 1646 case library, ~40 cases fully populated | 320 lb |
| Water tank (75 L full) | 165 lb |
| Marine battery (100 Ah AGM) | 70 lb |
| Solar panel (200 W rigid) | 25 lb |
| Personal effects + clothing | 80 lb |
| **Estimated total** | **~1,590 lb** |
| **Margin under 2,000 lb** | **~410 lb** |

The margin is workable but not generous. **Every added component requires weight accounting.** A second occupant's belongings would consume most of the remaining margin. A heavier battery system (lithium-iron-phosphate at ~150 lb for equivalent capacity, instead of 70 lb AGM) tightens it further. **Discipline matters.**

The implication for catalogue copy: **the Hako is sized for one occupant** as a base configuration. A two-person Hako exists but requires the customer to actively curate what travels with them.

## § 2 · Interior architecture · the four-wall plan

The 95" × 56" floor plan is essentially a long narrow rectangle — a "shotgun" interior. The architectural solution mirrors successful tiny-house and yacht interiors: **assign every wall a function**, leave only the center floor as movable circulation.

### 2.1 Wall assignments

```
       ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
   N   │  Murphy bed cabinet (6" deep stowed)    │ Far end
       │  Folds down to fill the room at night   │
       ├─────────────────┬───────────────────────┤
       │                 │                       │
       │   GALLEY WALL   │   passage             │
   E   │   12" deep      │   34" wide            │   W
       │   95" long      │   ~25 sq ft           │
       │   (cooking)     │                       │
       │                 │   KEEP STORAGE WALL   │
       │                 │   10" deep            │
       │                 │   95" long            │
       │                 │   (cell library)      │
       │                 │                       │
       ├─────────────────┴───────────────────────┤
   S   │  UTILITY WALL (12" deep)                │ Entry end
       │  Water tank, inverter, electrical,       │
       │  door (swings outward), control panel    │
       └─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **North short wall (far end, 56" W):** Murphy bed cabinet. 6" deep when bed is stowed. When deployed, the full-size mattress (54" × 75") drops into the room.
- **East long wall (95" L):** The galley wall. 12" deep built-in millwork housing the cooking microsystem.
- **West long wall (95" L):** KEEP storage. 10" deep floor-to-ceiling shelving for the 1646 case library.
- **South short wall (entry end, 56" W):** Utility wall. Door, water tank, inverter, electrical panel.

### 2.2 The central passage · 34" wide

Center clearance between galley (12" intrusion) and KEEP wall (10" intrusion) is **34" wide.** That's:
- Wider than ADA minimum corridor (32")
- Approximately yacht main-passage width
- Sufficient for one person to move freely; two people must coordinate

The full passage runs 95" long, gives about 25 sq ft of clear floor when the Murphy bed is stowed. **During the day this is the entire living/working/cooking space.** At night the Murphy bed drops and the floor disappears under the bed.

### 2.3 Galley wall · the microsystem deployment

The east wall is 95" long × 12" deep. Allocated as follows:

```
GALLEY WALL · 95" L × 12" D
├─ 0–24"     Murphy bed cabinet end (shared with bed cabinet)
├─ 24–60"    Cooking microsystem (36" run, the active zone)
│  ├─ Upper shelf (height 60-78"): iDOO GROW01 + LED clearance above
│  ├─ Counter (height 36"): cutting/prep + B&D RC503 + SPORE active tote
│  └─ Lower cabinets (0-36"): cell library access, drawers, garbage
├─ 60–84"    Sink + drainage zone (24" run)
│  ├─ Small bar-sink (~12" × 8"), drains to greywater holding
│  └─ Below sink: greywater tank + filter housing
└─ 84–95"    Compact appliance / storage end (11" run)
   └─ Could house compact fridge OR a tall pantry for STOCK refills
```

**Key dimensional notes:**

- **iDOO GROW01** (13.6" × 7.7") sits on the upper shelf with 14.88" of vertical clearance for the LED arm at full extension. Custom shelf cutout permits the arm to extend through to a higher cavity.
- **B&D RC503 rice cooker** (~8" × 8") sits on the counter when in active cook cycle. Stows on lower shelf otherwise.
- **SPORE active fruiting tote** (~14" × 8" × 6") sits on counter during the 2-week fruiting phase. Stows on a middle shelf during colonization (which needs darkness — perfect for an enclosed cabinet).
- **Sink** is small. Not a full kitchen sink. Bar-sized, sufficient for hand-washing, rinsing produce, filling pots, and cleaning small equipment. The water source is the utility-wall fresh tank; drainage goes to a greywater holding tank that the customer empties manually every few days.

### 2.4 KEEP storage wall · the cell library

The west wall is 95" long × 10" deep × ~78" tall (subtracting baseboard and ceiling reveal). That's enough for:

- **6 horizontal rows of 1646 case spaces** at 12" each = 72" vertical
- **Each row holds 2 cases side-by-side** (case width = 9-10")
- **Total cell capacity: ~12 cases × 16 cells = ~192 cells**

That's a meaningful library. The customer can hold:
- The complete BDRC 16-cell case
- The complete GROW 12-cell case
- The complete SPORE 12-cell catalogue
- A starter STOCK case (6-8 cuisine refills)
- Plus archive cells (personal KEEP cells — photos, documents, etc.) in the remaining capacity

**The west wall is the M1ND.studio catalogue made physical.** Every practice the studio offers is represented in the library. The customer's daily life pulls cells off this wall — a BDRC cell to cook with tonight, a GROW seed cell to start next month's growing cycle, a SPORE cell to begin a fresh mushroom cycle.

### 2.5 The utility wall · entry and infrastructure

The south wall (entry end) is 56" wide × 12" deep, housing:

- **Door** (swings outward, ~26" wide opening — minimum for moving large objects in/out)
- **Fresh water tank** (75 L / 20 gal) along one side of door
- **Greywater tank** (50 L / 13 gal) along other side
- **Electrical panel** (60" up): inverter mount, battery charge controller, switches
- **Control interface** (eye level): a small LCD or e-ink panel showing battery state, water levels, indoor temp/humidity, solar generation

The utility wall is dense. Every cubic inch earns its existence. The door swing eats some interior space when opening but otherwise the wall is functional storage and infrastructure.

## § 3 · The power architecture · AC bus from a fixed inverter

Inherited from GROW01-OG-spec-v0.2: a centralized AC bus from a fixed inverter, fed by a battery bank with solar charging. **The Hako interior is just an apartment from the power perspective.** Standard 110V receptacles, standard appliances, no DC-bus complexity.

### 3.1 The system, end to end

```
[200W roof solar panel]
        ↓ 18V DC
[MPPT charge controller, 30A] → [100 Ah AGM marine battery, 12V]
        ↓ 12V DC
[Pure-sine-wave inverter, 1000W continuous / 2000W surge]
        ↓ 110V AC
[Distribution: 4 standard outlets along walls]
        ├─ Galley wall: 2 outlets (iDOO, rice cooker, lights)
        ├─ KEEP wall: 1 outlet (work surface, laptop, phone)
        └─ Utility wall: 1 outlet (general purpose)
```

### 3.2 Why this specific configuration

- **100 Ah AGM marine battery, 12V nominal:** ~1,200 Wh nominal, ~900 Wh usable. AGM (absorbed glass mat) chosen over flooded lead-acid because the Hako moves — AGM is sealed and won't leak when transported. Chosen over lithium-iron-phosphate (LiFePO4) for weight/cost tradeoff: AGM is heavier (~70 lb) but ~half the price; LiFePO4 would save ~30 lb but add ~$800 to BOM. **At Hako BOM scale, AGM wins.** Upgrade path to LiFePO4 is straightforward if the customer wants it later.
- **1000W inverter:** Sized to run iDOO (40W AC) + rice cooker (350W AC) + small auxiliary loads simultaneously, with 2x surge headroom for cooker startup. **Per GROW01-OG-spec-v0.2, this is the right size.**
- **200W solar panel:** The largest single panel that fits on a U-Box roof. Mounting addressed in §3.4.
- **MPPT charge controller, 30A:** Higher rating than required (the 200W panel maxes around 12A at 18V) but the headroom matters for occasional dual-panel scenarios and for charging via wall plug when grid is available.

### 3.3 Daily energy budget

From the MICROSYSTEM-thread §4 + GROW01-OG-spec-v0.2 §3.3:

| Load | Daily Wh |
|---|---|
| iDOO GROW01 (continuous) | 474 Wh (via AC chain) |
| BDRC rice cooker (one meal/day) | 140 Wh |
| Interior LED lighting (4 hr) | 80 Wh |
| Phone/laptop charging | 50 Wh |
| Inverter idle + small loads | 60 Wh |
| **Total daily load** | **~800 Wh/day** |

| Solar input (Toronto, 200W panel) | Wh/day |
|---|---|
| Summer (June-August) | 640-800 Wh |
| Spring/Fall | 400-550 Wh |
| Winter (December-February) | 200-300 Wh |

**Conclusions:**
- **Summer:** Solar input matches or slightly exceeds daily load. **Indefinite off-grid operation is feasible.**
- **Spring/Fall:** Net deficit of ~300-400 Wh/day. Battery covers ~2 days of deficit before needing grid plug or reduced operation.
- **Winter:** Net deficit of ~500-600 Wh/day. **Hako requires grid plug-in or reduced microsystem operation (iDOO duty-cycled, fewer rice cooker uses).**

This is the most important honest disclosure in the spec. **The Hako is not full-time off-grid in Canadian winter without compromises.** Either accept reduced operation in winter, or supplement with grid plug-in when available, or upgrade to 400W solar (which doesn't physically fit on the U-Box roof without overhang structures), or accept the seasonal variability as part of the product.

The catalogue copy should say this directly: *"BXBX-005 operates indefinitely off-grid in Canadian summer and shoulder seasons. Winter operation typically requires grid plug-in 1-2x per week or reduced microsystem activity. Northern deployments and year-round indefinite off-grid require additional solar capacity beyond the base configuration."*

### 3.4 Solar mounting on the U-Box roof

The U-Box roof is approximately 95" × 56" = 37 sq ft of envelope-top area. A 200W rigid solar panel is typically ~60" × 30" = 12.5 sq ft. **The panel fits with room to spare**, mounted longitudinally with adhesive or rivet-on brackets. Cable routing through a roof-mounted weatherproof bulkhead connector to the interior charge controller.

**Important constraint:** the U-Box envelope must remain shippable. The solar panel cannot protrude beyond the envelope dimensions or it won't fit on the U-Box trailer. **The panel mounts flush to the roof, not on stand-offs.** This costs a few percent in efficiency (ideal angle for Toronto latitude is ~43° south-facing tilt; flat mounting loses ~15% of potential daily production) but preserves the U-Box-compatible shipping.

If the customer wants higher solar yield, the right approach is a **detachable supplementary panel** that travels inside the Hako during shipping and gets deployed on a stand once the Hako is sited. This adds ~100-200 W of additional capacity during stationary operation, removes for transit, and addresses the winter-deficit problem.

## § 4 · Water · the simplest viable system

Water in a tiny dwelling is genuinely the hardest infrastructure problem. The Hako solves it minimally.

### 4.1 The water architecture

```
[Fresh water tank, 75 L, in utility wall]
        ↓ gravity OR low-pressure pump
[Galley sink + iDOO refill point + drinking spigot]
        ↓ drainage
[Greywater holding tank, 50 L, in utility wall]
        ↓ manual emptying every 3-5 days
[External greywater disposal]
```

**No black water system.** The Hako does not have a toilet. **This is a deliberate design constraint.** Adding a toilet (composting, incinerating, or cassette-type) requires either:
- Adding 8-10 sq ft of floor (which the Hako doesn't have)
- Stealing it from the sleep zone (which makes the Hako uncomfortable)
- Adding a dedicated black-water tank with venting and disposal requirements (which complicates everything)

The honest answer: **The Hako assumes the occupant has access to nearby toilet facilities** — a host home, a campsite restroom, a coffee shop, a friend's apartment. The Hako is a sleeping/working/cooking/storing dwelling, not a fully self-contained dwelling like an RV. This is consistent with hostels, sleeping cabins, ryokan rooms, and yacht configurations where toilets are shared infrastructure outside the cabin.

Customers who require in-Hako toilet capability should look at a different BXBX variant (potentially a future BXBX-006 or larger).

### 4.2 Daily water use

| Use | Liters/day (1 person) |
|---|---|
| Drinking | 3 |
| Cooking | 1 |
| iDOO top-up | 0.5-1.5 (varies by growth cycle) |
| Misting (SPORE) + cleaning | 0.5 |
| Hand-washing | 0.5 |
| **Total** | **5.5-6.5 L/day** |

**Tank duration: 75 L / 6 L per day ≈ 12-13 days** per fill.

**Greywater duration:** roughly the same input minus what evaporates from the iDOO. ~50 L tank holds ~10 days of greywater before requiring emptying.

Both tanks have visible fill-level indicators on the utility wall control panel. The customer empties greywater whenever convenient — a 5-gallon bucket carry to the host site's drain, or a dedicated greywater dispersion bed if the Hako is permanently sited.

### 4.3 Water refill

The fresh water tank fills via an exterior threaded inlet on the utility wall, accessible from outside. Standard garden-hose thread (GHT). 75 L fills in ~5 minutes from any standard outdoor spigot. The customer can carry water to the Hako in jerry cans if no nearby spigot is available — 75 L is roughly four 5-gallon jerry can loads.

## § 5 · Ventilation and climate

A 257 cu ft sealed envelope with a human and an active hydroponic system inside needs ventilation. The Hako solves it minimally.

### 5.1 Air exchange requirements

A single occupant consumes ~25 L of oxygen per hour and exhales ~25 L of CO2. In a 257 cu ft / 7,280 L sealed space, CO2 builds up to uncomfortable levels (>1000 ppm) within ~3 hours of single occupancy without ventilation.

**Solution: a low-power roof-mounted ventilation fan + passive intake.** Specifically:

- **Maxxair MaxxFan Deluxe** or equivalent 12V roof fan, ~3" tall above roof line (the only acceptable U-Box-compatible roof protrusion besides the solar panel)
- Continuous low-speed mode draws ~2A at 12V (~24W = ~600 Wh/day)
- Variable speed for cooking events (rice cooker steam) and high-humidity SPORE fruiting periods
- Passive intake vent on opposite wall (low position) provides cross-flow

**Power budget impact:** The 24W continuous fan adds to the daily 800 Wh load, pushing it to ~1,400 Wh/day total. This significantly worsens the off-grid power picture documented in §3.3 — summer operation becomes barely-positive, shoulder seasons net-negative, winter strongly net-negative.

**Mitigation:** Run the fan in cycled mode (15 min on / 45 min off) instead of continuous. Air exchange drops to ~25% but the dwelling remains habitable for single occupancy. Power consumption drops to ~150 Wh/day. **This is the studio's recommended default mode** with manual override when needed.

### 5.2 Heating and cooling

- **Summer cooling:** Reflective roof + light-colored exterior + roof fan + careful sun-orientation when siting. **No active AC.** Adding AC adds 1,000+ W of intermittent load which the off-grid power can't support without massive battery+solar upgrades. Customer accepts that Hako summers may run warm.
- **Winter heating:** A small 12V or 110V space heater used briefly to bring interior to comfortable temperature before sleep, then occupant body heat + insulation maintains it overnight. **Resistive heat (electric) is brutal on the power budget.** A propane heater (Mr. Buddy or similar) is the realistic option but introduces venting and CO concerns. **Honest spec:** the Hako is comfortable in shoulder seasons; summer is warm; deep winter requires grid plug-in for electric heat or supplementary propane.

The Hako is not climate-conditioned to apartment standards. It's closer to a well-insulated cabin: comfortable in temperate weather, requires occupant adaptation in extreme weather.

### 5.3 Insulation

The U-Box steel-frame construction is essentially uninsulated as shipped. The Hako interior build adds insulation between the U-Box skin and interior finishes. Recommended: **rigid foam board (2" thick, R-10) on walls and ceiling, ~1" under floor**. This costs ~40-50 lb in weight, adds ~3" of interior reduction on each surface (already accounted for in §1.2 headroom math), and roughly halves heat-transfer rates versus uninsulated. **For Canadian deployment, this insulation level is the minimum acceptable.**

## § 6 · The lived experience · a day in the Hako

To make this real, an honest sketch of what daily life in the Hako looks like once deployed:

**Morning, ~7am.** Wake to gentle morning light through the small east-facing window above the galley wall (or, in winter, to the LED grow light on the iDOO already in its 16-hour photoperiod). The Murphy bed folds up against the north wall, revealing the day's living floor. Brush teeth at the bar sink. Walk the ~30 seconds to the host site's bathroom.

**Breakfast.** Toggle the B&D rice cooker with a BDRC steel-cut-oats variant (Cell 17 from the proposed catalogue expansion in BDRC-recipes-v0.2 Q2). Walk away. Check the iDOO — water level fine, plants growing. Check the SPORE tote in the galley cabinet — colonization on schedule. Total active engagement: 3 minutes.

**Work.** Pull the work-surface flap down from the KEEP wall (a fold-down desk built into the cell shelving). Sit on the small stool stored against the utility wall. Laptop on the surface, plugged into the KEEP-wall outlet, which is fed by the inverter from the battery. Work happens.

**Lunch.** The oats are long done. Eat them with fresh herbs snipped from the iDOO and a finishing drizzle of olive oil from a STOCK cell.

**Afternoon.** Continue working. Or step outside the Hako entirely — the dwelling is small enough that "going outside" isn't optional, it's structurally required. Walk to a coffee shop, sit in a park, run errands.

**Evening.** Cook dinner. Pull the BDRC Mushroom Farro cell, pull the SPORE oyster mushrooms (harvested earlier this week, kept in the small under-counter fridge if present, or used fresh-cut from the fruiting tote), pull the GROW herbs at the moment of plating. The 30-minute cook cycle runs while reading on the small stool.

**Night.** Drop the Murphy bed. Dim the LEDs. Sleep.

**Repeat for as long as the Hako stays sited at this location.** When it's time to move: empty greywater, drain fresh water tank (or transport partial), secure all loose objects, load onto a U-Box trailer, transit, redeploy at next location.

**This is the lived form of the studio's thesis.** Commodity envelope. Designed interior. Every appliance has its specified place. Every catalogue practice has its purpose served. Customer's life accumulates in the cells on the KEEP wall, plays out on the working floor, and goes off-grid for weeks at a time.

## § 8 · What this enables · the catalogue capstone

### 8.1 The flagship product

The BXBX-005 Hako with full microsystem deployment is the studio's catalogue capstone — the product that demonstrates **everything M1ND.studio does, in one shippable object.** Every practice represented. Every commodity envelope thesis vindicated. Every editorial design decision made tangible.

This positions the Hako differently from any single-practice product:

- **A BDRC customer** buys a $50 cell to cook a meal
- **A KEEP customer** buys 1646 cases to organize their archives  
- **A GROW customer** buys an iDOO to grow herbs
- **A Hako customer** buys the whole studio operating system as a physical dwelling

These are different customers, different price points, and different intent levels. The Hako customer is the most committed and is also the customer for whom the studio has the most to offer. **One Hako contains roughly $1,000-1,500 of studio-catalogue cells in its initial library**, and adds another $50-300/month as the customer cycles through new cells over time. **The Hako is the studio's highest-LTV product by an order of magnitude.**

### 8.2 The catalogue argument made physical

A skeptic asks: *"What is M1ND.studio?"* The studio's prior answers have been editorial — research documents, recipe specs, design threads, philosophy. **The Hako is the studio's first physical answer.** Walk into a Hako, see the full catalogue deployed, and the question is answered without further discussion.

This matters for sales positioning, for press coverage, for cultural placement. A single completed BXBX-005 Hako, photographed and toured, becomes the studio's most defensible marketing artifact. **The Hako is the studio's manifesto.**

### 8.3 What it doesn't try to be

In the spirit of every prior thread document, worth declaring what the Hako is NOT:

- **Not an RV.** RVs are road-traveling dwellings with their own engines and full plumbing. The Hako is a tiny house shipped on a trailer to a fixed site, not driven daily.
- **Not a tiny home in the HGTV sense.** Those are typically larger (200+ sq ft), more amenity-rich, less mobile, more expensive.
- **Not a full alternative to apartment living.** No toilet, no laundry, no shower (a future Hako variant might add an outdoor shower module). The Hako requires nearby infrastructure for those needs.
- **Not built for couples.** Single-occupant is the design target. Two-person is possible but compromised.
- **Not fully off-grid in Canadian winter** without supplementation (per §3.3).
- **Not legally a "permitted dwelling"** under most Canadian zoning codes. It's a tiny shelter on a moveable trailer — closer to a permitted accessory structure or a temporary structure than to a code-compliant permanent dwelling. **The customer is responsible for sitting it on legally-appropriate land.**

These are deliberate scope decisions. The Hako does specific things very well within its envelope. It does not try to be all things to all customers.

## § 10 · Open questions for next session

**Q1 · The single biggest design risk:** the 34" central passage during day mode and the no-floor situation when the Murphy bed is down. **Is this actually livable, or is the Hako too small to be honest about?** Real bench testing — building a mockup at studio scale or doing a few nights of sleep-in-the-current-studio simulation — would answer this before committing to a build. *Recommended Phase 0 first activity: a 4'×8' floor mockup at the Dupont studio with the proposed layout taped out, lived-in for a weekend.*

**Q2 · The toilet question, reopened.** A Hako without a toilet is honest and simpler, but it constrains the customer base significantly. **Is there a Hako variant that includes a composting toilet?** Probably not BXBX-005 (no floor area available), but possibly a BXBX-005·plus with a slight footprint extension. Worth thinking through whether the spec should commit to "no toilet, ever, in this variant" or leave the door open.

**Q3 · The U-Box procurement question.** U-Haul rents U-Boxes by the month but doesn't typically sell them. **Where does the studio actually obtain the U-Box envelope?** Long-term lease arrangements with U-Haul? Direct purchase from U-Haul (possible, with negotiation)? Used U-Boxes from auctions or end-of-fleet sales? A 3D-printed or custom-built equivalent envelope at the same dimensions? **Real research needed before Phase 0 begins.** The new steel U-Box model (introduced 2026) might be more amenable to purchase than the legacy plywood version.

**Q4 · Zoning and legal positioning.** As noted in §8.3, the Hako exists in a regulatory grey zone for permitted Canadian dwellings. **The studio's catalogue copy needs careful language** — "shelter," "studio space," "accessory structure," "tiny dwelling," etc. all carry different regulatory implications. Worth a real conversation with a small-property lawyer in Ontario before public catalogue launch.

**Q5 · The 400W solar question.** Per §3.3, 200W solar is summer-indefinite but winter-deficit. **Is a 400W detachable supplementary panel the right answer, or should the catalogue have a separate "winter package" with permanent 400W panels via an articulated mount?** The articulated mount adds complexity and weight; the detachable supplementary panel adds setup labor on every redeploy. **A real design decision worth modeling more carefully.**

**Q6 · The first-customer profile.** Who exactly buys the first BXBX-005 Hako? At $20-28K all-in, this is not an impulse purchase. Customer archetypes worth scoping:
- The accessory-dwelling-unit customer (existing homeowner adding a backyard Hako for a parent / adult child / income)
- The land-rich-cash-poor rural property owner (someone with land but no buildings on it)
- The professional sailing-or-vanlife customer wanting a fixed base
- The artist-in-residence-on-someone-else's-property customer
- The remote worker shifting locations seasonally
- The climate-resilience customer wanting a self-sufficient backup dwelling

Each archetype shapes the catalogue copy differently. **My read:** the studio's strongest first-customer profile is the ADU customer with existing property — already invested in their location, looking for a small-footprint expansion, willing to spend ~$20-30K on a single highly-designed object. The other archetypes are real but more specialized.
