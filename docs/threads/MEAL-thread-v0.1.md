## § 1 · MEAL's domain

MEAL covers the research and editorial side of:

- **The contemporary food system** — supply chains, ingredient availability, urban food economics, the gap between what's actually possible at home and what's commercially marketed
- **Domestic-scale food production** — what individuals or households can produce themselves with reasonable effort, the technologies and infrastructures that make small-scale production work
- **Culinary systems and methodology** — how cooking practices interact with sourcing, storage, and dwelling; how recipes function as documents and how recipe-catalogues function as systems
- **The studio's specific contribution** — the STOCK family + sub-lines (COOK, BDRC, GROW, SPORE) as one cohesive answer to the broader questions MEAL investigates

The practice's editorial register inherits from MEAL.0001: **consulting-grade research writing, analytically serious, opinionated where opinion is earned, illustrated with real numbers and real-world examples rather than aspirational lifestyle imagery.** Same register as T1NY and MHYC; different unit of analysis.

### What MEAL is NOT

- **Not a recipe blog.** Recipes live in STOCK and its sub-lines as products. MEAL is the research layer that contextualizes the recipes, not the recipes themselves.
- **Not a food media outlet in the lifestyle register.** *Bon Appetit* / *Saveur* / *NYT Cooking* serve a different audience. MEAL's audience reads to understand, not to be entertained.
- **Not a "back to the land" or anti-modernity project.** MEAL doesn't argue people should grow all their own food or abandon supermarkets. It analyzes what's *worth* producing at domestic scale and why, with the studio's catalogue as one specific answer.
- **Not Toronto-only.** Like T1NY and MHYC, MEAL operates with global frame and Toronto vantage point.

## § 2 · The founding thesis · what MEAL.0001 established

*(Brief recap — MEAL.0001 itself is the canonical statement. This section summarizes what readers of this thread doc need to know.)*

MEAL.0001's founding thesis can be stated as three interconnected claims:

**1. The food system has fragmented.** What used to be a single "food economy" — local farmers, neighborhood butchers, corner grocers, home cooking — has split into multiple parallel systems (supermarket, restaurant, meal kit, takeout, ghost kitchen, direct-to-consumer specialty). Each system serves a niche; none serves the household well end-to-end. The household has to operate across all of them and orchestrate the result.

**2. Domestic-scale production has become technologically feasible at a higher level than most people realize.** Hydroponic systems, mushroom cultivation, fermentation, sourdough — all have crossed accessibility thresholds in the last 5-10 years. **A motivated household can produce 20-40% of its fresh ingredients at home** with technology that costs less than a year's CSA subscription. Most people don't know this.

**3. The studio's catalogue is one specific synthesis** of available technologies into a coherent system. Not the only synthesis, but a designed one: BDRC for grain meals, GROW for fresh herbs and greens, SPORE for mushrooms, STOCK for cuisine refills, with shared infrastructure (Wowlive shelving, 1646 cells, KEEP storage) and shared power architecture (Ryobi inverter ecosystem). **The system is the product**, not any individual cell.

MEAL.0001 documents these claims with real data, real cost analysis, real case studies. **Future MEAL.NNNN reports extend the investigation** along specific axes (see § 4).

## § 3 · Relationship to STOCK and the food build family

MEAL produces editorial research. STOCK + its sub-lines produce physical products. The two practices interact deliberately:

| Direction | What flows |
|---|---|
| **MEAL → STOCK** | Research surfaces gaps in the catalogue, ingredient sourcing opportunities, cuisines worth adding, technologies worth incorporating |
| **STOCK → MEAL** | Product launches and customer feedback inform what's worth deeper editorial investigation; specific cells become case studies in MEAL reports |
| **MEAL ↔ readers** | Editorial readers become catalogue customers; catalogue customers become editorial subscribers; the two audiences overlap substantially |

This is the same dynamic as T1NY:BXBX (research informs builds, builds become subjects of research) and MHYC:MESH (research surfaces in the matching tool, matching tool generates new research questions). **Consistent architecture across the three research:build pairs.**

### How the customer encounters both

Public catalogue at m1nd.co lists practices clearly. A reader interested in food can enter through either:

- **stock.m1nd.co** (or subdomain equivalent) — the product catalogue with COOK / BDRC / GROW / SPORE cells available for purchase
- **meal.m1nd.co** (or subdomain equivalent) — the research publication with reports, analyses, and editorial pieces

Each cross-links to the other naturally. A STOCK product page links to its relevant MEAL coverage; a MEAL report on hydroponic herbs links to the GROW cell catalogue. **Two surfaces, one coherent food practice.**

### A small structural question: should MEAL share stock.m1nd.co or have its own subdomain?

**My read: own subdomain (meal.m1nd.co), same as T1NY/MHYC.** Reasons:

1. Architectural symmetry — the other research practices have own subdomains
2. Editorial autonomy — MEAL's research register is distinct from STOCK's product register
3. Audience separation — readers of consulting-grade food research are not always catalogue customers (yet); MEAL's growth path benefits from its own surface
4. Operational simplicity — MESH manages all the subdomains via the same Ghost stack; one more subdomain is trivial

The catalogue map locks meal.m1nd.co alongside the other research practice surfaces.

## § 4 · Future MEAL.NNNN report candidates

MEAL.0001 established the practice. MEAL.0002 and beyond extend the investigation. Working candidates:

### MEAL.0002 · The Toronto Importer Landscape

A field-research study of Toronto's specialty food importers — the businesses that bring in saffron, North African spice blends, Asian dry goods, European pasta and cheese, and the long tail of regional ingredients that supermarkets don't carry. **Critical for STOCK's sourcing strategy.**

Format: directory + analytical commentary. Lists ~30-50 importers with locations, specialties, accessibility (public-facing or B2B-only), pricing observations, ethical/origin notes. Analytical sections cover the structural patterns — which immigrant communities support which import flows, how the city's food ethnography maps onto specific neighborhoods, what's emerging vs declining.

Estimated effort: ~80-100 hours over 2-3 months. **Real-world value to the studio: high** — this becomes the working document for STOCK Phase 1 sourcing.

### MEAL.0003 · The Microsystem Case Studies

Documented case studies of the studio's microsystem deployment in real settings — the studio's own Dupont Arts kitchen, Jordan's home kitchen, eventually customer kitchens. Each case study covers:

- The physical setup (what cells, what shelving, what spatial arrangement)
- The operational reality (what actually got used, what didn't, what the customer learned)
- The economic math (what it cost, what it saved, what it would cost to replicate today)
- The customer's honest assessment (what they'd change, what they'd recommend)

Estimated effort: ~40-60 hours per case study, 3-5 case studies per year once customer deployments exist.

### MEAL.0004 · Off-Grid Food at Domestic Scale

A deeper study of off-grid food production, building on the MICROSYSTEM-thread and GROW01-OG-spec work. Covers:

- The Ryobi-powered microsystem (full energy analysis, expanded from GROW01-OG-spec)
- Solar-supplemented operation patterns (the actual seasonal math)
- Cabin / RV / Hako deployment cases
- What's actually possible vs marketing claims in the off-grid food space

Estimated effort: ~60-80 hours.

### MEAL.0005 · The CFIA Compliance Landscape for Small Food Producers

Practical research on Canadian Food Inspection Agency requirements as they apply to studios, small producers, and cottage-industry operators. Covers:

- SFCR registration, license requirements, exemptions
- Front-of-package nutrition labelling (the 2026 rules)
- Provincial variation
- What's actually required vs commonly believed
- Cost and time investment for a typical small food business

Estimated effort: ~40 hours. **Genuinely useful for the studio and for any similar operator reading.**

### MEAL.0006 · A Year of the BDRC Catalogue

A comprehensive customer-facing assessment of the BDRC sub-line one year post-launch. What sold, what didn't, what customers actually cooked, what they substituted, what they returned. Honest assessment of the studio's own product line. **Models the kind of self-evaluation the studio practices.**

Estimated effort: ~30-40 hours, contingent on BDRC being live for a year first.

### Longer-horizon report candidates

- **MEAL.0007:** Food cost trajectories in Canada 2020-2030 — analysis of where prices have gone, what's coming
- **MEAL.0008:** The cultural-cuisine catalogue problem — how to build a respectful food catalogue spanning cuisines from cultures the studio doesn't operate within
- **MEAL.0009:** Composting and waste loops at domestic scale — the closing of the studio's microsystem
- **MEAL.0010+:** Open

**Publishing rhythm:** ~2-3 major reports per year once MEAL is established as a continuous practice. This is similar to MEAL.0001's effort level applied repeatedly, sustainable alongside the studio's other work.

## § 5 · Publishing format and operations

### 5.1 · Format

MEAL reports follow MEAL.0001's established format:

- **Long-form consulting-grade research writing** (~10-20K words per major report)
- **Real data, real numbers, real cost analysis** where applicable
- **Beautifully-typeset web version + downloadable PDF**
- **Stable URLs** under meal.m1nd.co for archive integrity
- **Citations and source documentation** for every claim that needs supporting
- **Studio voice** — opinionated, analytical, willing to take positions, occasionally dryly funny

### 5.2 · Publishing infrastructure

- **Domain:** meal.m1nd.co
- **Platform:** Ghost (shared stack with t1ny.m1nd.co, mhyc.m1nd.co)
- **Design system:** inherits 90% from T1NY's research-publication system, with accent color differentiated (working idea: warm-tone accent to signal food register, paired with the studio's paper-and-ink foundation)
- **Email subscriptions:** Yes, via Ghost. Major-report cadence (~2-3/year) gives subscribers reason to maintain interest without being spammed.

### 5.3 · Editorial standards

- Every claim that depends on data gets a real source citation
- Every cost figure gets a date and a sourcing note (prices change)
- Every product or business named gets fair treatment — credit for what's good, honest noting of what's not
- Pseudonyms allowed for case-study customers who request them; never invented or composite
- The studio's own products covered honestly, including their weaknesses

## § 6 · Operating questions

**Q1 · MEAL.0001's status and refresh cycle.** The inaugural report is published but predates the full catalogue scaffolding work of May 2026. **Does MEAL.0001 get a v1.1 refresh that incorporates the catalogue developments since publication?** My read: leave MEAL.0001 as the historical document it is; the catalogue developments get their own future MEAL reports. Reports don't get retconned; they get updated companions.

**Q2 · Editorial bandwidth.** Producing 2-3 major MEAL reports per year is real work — ~150-300 hours annually across the practice. **Is this sustainable alongside T1NY, MHYC, and the build practices?** My read: yes, but only if MEAL reports are paced realistically (no Q4-2026 deadline pressure on MEAL.0002; ship when ready). The studio shouldn't take on report obligations it can't meet without compromising other work.

**Q3 · Contributor / guest-author capacity.** Some MEAL topics benefit from outside expertise — the CFIA compliance research (MEAL.0005) might be co-authored with a small-food-business consultant; the cultural cuisine work (MEAL.0008) absolutely needs co-authors from the specific cultures discussed. **What's the studio's approach to commissioning?** My read: pay industry-standard rates ($1,000-3,000 per major contribution depending on scope), maintain editorial direction in-house, credit contributors prominently. Same pattern as MHYC's contributor approach.

**Q4 · The meal.m1nd.co subdomain priority.** Multiple subdomains queued for Phase 0 setup. **What's MEAL's priority relative to T1NY and MHYC?** My read: lower urgency than T1NY/MHYC because MEAL.0001 already exists as a published artifact (currently lives wherever it was originally posted) and the active publishing pace will be slower. **Phase 1 timing is fine.** T1NY/MHYC need their subdomains first because they're scaffolding from scratch.

**Q5 · Cross-reference policy.** MEAL reports will naturally cite STOCK products, T1NY dwelling research, MHYC neighborhood profiles, and (eventually) other MEAL reports. **Should the studio establish a citation convention?** My read: yes, simple footnote-style with full URLs for permanence. Same approach as MEAL.0001 already uses.

**Q6 · The food-research-vs-cooking-content question.** The line between MEAL (research) and STOCK product copy (catalogue) needs care. **Where does "how to cook a BDRC cell" content live?** My read: cooking instructions live inside the BDRC cells themselves (booklet card per cell, per existing BDRC spec); MEAL reports may *reference* cooking practices when analytically relevant but don't reproduce recipes. **Keep the registers separate.**

## § 7 · Strategic position

### 7.1 · MEAL completes the catalogue's research counterweight

The studio's catalogue has two strong build axes: dwelling (BXBX + Hako) and food (STOCK + sub-lines). **Without MEAL formally articulated, the food side lacked its editorial counterweight** that the dwelling side already has via T1NY. Formalizing MEAL fixes that asymmetry.

### 7.2 · MEAL credibility supports STOCK sales

A customer evaluating a BDRC cell at $50 needs context. *"Why does a packaged grain meal cost $50?"* is a real question. **MEAL.0002 (Toronto importer landscape) provides part of the answer** — readers see what the underlying ingredient sourcing actually looks like, what the studio is paying its specialty importers, why mass-market alternatives can't replicate the cell.

This is the same architecture as T1NY supporting BXBX (research context makes the dwelling product legible) and MHYC supporting MESH (editorial substance makes the matching tool credible). **Research underwrites product across all three pairs.**

### 7.3 · MEAL opens consulting work

The studio's editorial credibility in food research opens consulting engagements with:
- Small food producers navigating CFIA compliance
- Restaurant operators considering retail product lines
- Cookware / kitchen-appliance brands developing micro-production tools
- Municipalities or non-profits working on food security at neighborhood scale

This consulting work pays better than retail STOCK sales and is the practice's likely operating-revenue engine through Phase 1-2.

### 7.4 · MEAL future-proofs the food practice

If STOCK sales plateau or shift, MEAL's research practice retains independent value. **The studio's bet on food is robust** regardless of any specific product's commercial trajectory because the underlying research has documentary worth.
