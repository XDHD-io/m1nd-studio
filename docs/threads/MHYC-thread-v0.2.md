## § 0 · What changed since v0.1

One architectural correction:

**The matching tool ownership moves from MHYC to MESH.** v0.1 placed the matching tool inside MHYC as the inaugural interactive product, which created a structural inconsistency — MHYC was research-and-editorial but suddenly also producing software. With MESH now properly named as the studio's digital build practice (MESH-thread-v0.1), the cleaner architecture is:

- **MHYC produces** the neighborhood research, the editorial profiles, the curated match commentary, the geo-arbitrage analyses, the field reports
- **MESH builds** the interactive matching tool at mhyc.m1nd.co/match that operationalizes MHYC's research
- **The two practices collaborate** on the matching tool — MESH owns the build, MHYC owns the editorial layer (which matches are confirmed, which are adjacent, what commentary accompanies each)

This mirrors the established T1NY:BXBX pattern (T1NY produces research, BXBX builds dwellings) at the geographic/digital scale (MHYC produces research, MESH builds tools). **Same architectural pattern, two scales of operation.**

Everything else in v0.1 holds. MHYC's founding thesis, publishing products, register, and operating questions remain unchanged. The revision is surgical.

## § 1 · What MHYC is

**MHYC is the studio's continuous research and editorial practice covering the global network of culturally-coherent neighborhoods and how people increasingly live across them.** Where T1NY asks *"given that you need to live somewhere, what does that somewhere look like?"* — a dwelling-scale question — MHYC asks *"given that the world is full of neighborhoods, which ones are yours and how do you move between them?"* — a geography-scale question.

The publishing surface (probably **mhyc.m1nd.co**) produces:

- **Neighborhood profiles** — editorial deep-dives on currently-interesting neighborhoods worldwide, written as travel-guide-meets-cultural-criticism
- **Match commentary** — the editorial layer that confirms which neighborhoods match which (the underlying interactive tool that surfaces matches is built by MESH; the editorial judgment about *which* matches are real lives here)
- **Geo-arbitrage analyses** — for specific city pairs or rotational patterns, what's the actual math? Cost of living, visa friction, internet quality, climate, community access
- **Digital nomadism field reports** — practical assessments of specific cities/neighborhoods as bases for distributed work
- **Cross-pollination essays** — what one neighborhood's culture has taught another, or could teach another

MHYC is consciously **research- and editorial-first**. The interactive matching tool (built by MESH) is the *jumping-in surface*, but MHYC's editorial publication is the *substance*.

### What MHYC is NOT

- **Not a "best cities for digital nomads" listicle factory.** That genre is everywhere, mostly thin, and reliably dated within months. MHYC's register is editorial-depth: profiles that hold up for years, analyses that get rewritten when conditions change rather than published as one-off ranked lists.
- **Not a relocation services brand.** MHYC doesn't help people move; it helps people *think about* moving. The relocation industry has its own players (Sondering, Selina, Plumia, Outsite) and MHYC's value proposition is upstream — at the *understanding* stage, before the *acting* stage.
- **Not a real estate or short-term rental platform.** MHYC doesn't list properties. The neighborhood is the unit of analysis, not the apartment.
- **Not a software product practice.** That's MESH. MHYC produces research; MESH builds the tools that operationalize it.

## § 2 · The founding thesis · neighborhoods are the world's actual unit of meaning

*(Unchanged from v0.1.)*

People don't really live in *cities* — they live in *neighborhoods*. A New Yorker who lives in the Lower East Side has more in common with a Berliner who lives in Friedrichshain than with a New Yorker who lives in the Upper East Side. **The neighborhood is the cultural and economic unit that actually shapes daily life**, and cities are largely administrative wrappers around very different neighborhoods that share a tax base.

This has always been true but is becoming increasingly *legible* as more people live across multiple places. Remote work has decoupled job from residence. The pandemic normalized location flexibility for a significant slice of professional work. Visa regimes for digital nomads now exist in 60+ countries. Geo-arbitrage — earning in a strong currency while living in a weaker one — has moved from edge-of-society practice into mainstream financial planning advice for younger professionals.

In this world, the question *"where do you live?"* is increasingly inadequate. The honest answers are now:

- *"Six months in Lisbon, three in Mexico City, three in Toronto with family."*
- *"Toronto's my base but I'm rotating through Berlin twice a year."*
- *"My partner is in Singapore, I'm splitting between there and Bangkok depending on visas."*
- *"I gave up a Toronto apartment two years ago, now I rotate three neighborhoods I know well."*

**These patterns are not exotic anymore.** They are how a growing minority of working people in their 20s-40s actually organize their lives. And the practical question this minority encounters constantly is: *"I love how I live in [my hood] — what does that translate to in [your city]?"*

MHYC exists to answer that question seriously. The neighborhood is the unit. The match is the editorial artifact. The interactive matching tool (built by MESH) makes the match findable.

### The Anglosphere-anomaly thesis, neighborhood edition

T1NY's founding thesis is that the Anglosphere's suburban-detached-home pattern is a 75-year historical anomaly against the global norm of dense urban dwelling. MHYC has a parallel thesis at the neighborhood scale:

**The Anglosphere's habit of treating cities as monolithic — "I live in Toronto," "I live in Brooklyn" — is itself a flattening that hides the actual texture of where people live.** In Tokyo, Bangkok, Berlin, Lisbon, Mexico City, the neighborhood is the operative unit of identity and culture. You don't live in Tokyo; you live in Shimokitazawa or Nakameguro or Koenji, and those are *radically different places* with different demographics, different rhythms, different aesthetics, different daily logistics. **The Anglosphere has neighborhoods too, of course — but the culture often treats them as variations on a city theme rather than as the primary units of meaning.**

MHYC takes the global view seriously. The matching tool and the editorial profiles operate at neighborhood granularity because that's where actual meaning lives.

### The geo-arbitrage thesis

Layered on top of the neighborhood-as-meaning thesis: **for a growing minority of professionals, the economic argument for living in a single high-cost-of-living city full-time has eroded.** If your work is location-flexible and your relationships permit some travel, the math of rotating between two or three cities (one expensive home base + one or two affordable rotation cities) often outperforms full-time residence in any single high-cost city. This isn't speculation; it's documented in the cost-of-living analyses underlying digital nomad communities since roughly 2015.

The arbitrage gets stronger when you choose neighborhoods that match your actual taste. Living in *bad* Lisbon is not better than living in Toronto; living in *the specific Lisbon neighborhood that matches your taste* is meaningfully different and often genuinely better. **The matching is what unlocks the arbitrage.** MHYC's research and MESH's tool together make the matching feasible at scale.

## § 3 · MHYC's three inaugural research products

*(Slightly trimmed from v0.1 — the matching tool moves out as it's now a MESH product. Three remain as MHYC's direct editorial outputs.)*

### 3.1 · Neighborhood Profiles · the editorial workhorse

Long-form essays profiling specific neighborhoods, ~3,500-5,000 words each. Format models: *The New Yorker*'s long city pieces, *The Atlantic*'s travel writing, *Roads & Kingdoms* at its best, *Atlas Obscura*'s longform side. Honest about both the appeal and the friction. Includes:

- The neighborhood's current cultural moment (who's there, why, what's emerging)
- The historical context (how the neighborhood became what it is)
- The practical math (rent, transit, internet, food, climate)
- The community access points (where newcomers can plug in)
- The match notes (which neighborhoods elsewhere this one resembles, and why) — *this is the editorial content that feeds into MESH's matching tool*

Inaugural neighborhood targets (working list, expandable):
- **Lower East Side, NYC** — the studio's canonical example
- **Friedrichshain, Berlin** — Lower East Side's most cited analogue
- **Lavapiés, Madrid** — Spanish-speaking analogue with strong North African / Latin American presence
- **Shimokitazawa, Tokyo** — Japan's most LES-adjacent neighborhood
- **Roma Norte, Mexico City** — the canonical CDMX nomad neighborhood, increasingly its own thing
- **Príncipe Real, Lisbon** — the current darling of European digital nomadism
- **Trinity Bellwoods area, Toronto** — the studio's home, written from the inside
- **Chiado / Bairro Alto, Lisbon** — adjacent comparison piece
- **Marais, Paris** — older neighborhood with current moment
- **Williamsburg, Brooklyn** — for the matching contrast against LES
- **Kreuzberg, Berlin** — for the matching contrast against Friedrichshain
- **Songshan / Da'an, Taipei** — under-covered Asian options

**Publishing rhythm:** roughly 2-3 profiles per quarter, building toward ~30-50 profiles over the practice's first three years. **Each profile becomes a reference document** that gets updated periodically rather than going stale.

### 3.2 · Geo-Arbitrage Analyses · the practical math

Periodic deep dives on specific rotational patterns or city pairs. ~2,500-3,500 words each. The genre is something like investment-research-meets-travel-writing: serious about the numbers, alive to the human texture. Examples:

- *"The Toronto / Mexico City Rotation in 2026"* — actual costs, actual visa friction, actual relationship logistics, actual seasonal calendar
- *"Lisbon Is Done · what comes after"* — when a digital nomad city becomes too expensive or too saturated, where do people go next?
- *"The Triangle: Berlin / Lisbon / Mexico City as a Year"* — the standard European-North-American digital nomad rotation, scrutinized
- *"East Asian Bases for Western Workers in 2026"* — Taipei, Bangkok, Chiang Mai, Bali, Da Nang compared

**Publishing rhythm:** ~4 per year, deliberately. These are research-heavy and benefit from being timely rather than continuous.

### 3.3 · Digital Nomadism Field Reports · the practitioner perspective

The most-likely-to-be-co-authored product. Practitioners actively living distributed lives contribute reports on what's currently working in specific neighborhoods or cities — the on-the-ground assessment of internet quality, community access, café culture, visa logistics, neighborhood-specific gotchas.

**Publishing rhythm:** opportunistic — when good contributors emerge with genuine field knowledge. Not on a schedule.

## § 4 · Relationship to the studio's full catalogue · sister practices

MHYC has two distinct relationships to other studio practices that need naming clearly:

### 4.1 · MHYC and T1NY · sister research practices

Both are research-and-editorial practices, distinguished by unit of analysis:

| Practice | Unit of analysis | Founding question |
|---|---|---|
| **T1NY** | The dwelling | *"What does the dwelling itself look like?"* |
| **MHYC** | The neighborhood | *"Where is your dwelling and how do you move?"* |

Both operate from the Anglosphere-anomaly thesis at different scales. Both serve readers thinking seriously about non-conventional dwelling and living patterns. **A T1NY reader planning a stealth-RV-in-Lisbon setup needs MHYC to know which Lisbon neighborhood. An MHYC reader planning a three-city rotation needs T1NY to know what dwelling pattern works in each location.** Sister research practices, mutually reinforcing.

### 4.2 · MHYC and MESH · research / build partnership

MHYC produces the editorial substance. MESH builds the digital products that operationalize it. **The matching tool at mhyc.m1nd.co/match is the canonical example:**

- **MHYC owns:** which neighborhoods get profiled, which matches are confirmed (Strong / Adjacent / Aspirational), what commentary accompanies each match, the editorial standards that distinguish a credible match from a thin one
- **MESH owns:** the technical build of the matching tool, the map UI, the vector-embedding pipeline for candidate generation, the deployment and maintenance of mhyc.m1nd.co/match as a working web product
- **Both collaborate:** the editorial-content flow from MHYC into the tool, the design choices that make the editorial register feel right inside the tool's interface

This division mirrors T1NY:BXBX where T1NY produces research and BXBX builds dwellings. Same architecture, two scales (dwelling-scale vs digital-product-scale).

### 4.3 · The catalogue 2×2

Per MESH-thread v0.1:

| | Research practice | Build practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical / dwelling-scale** | T1NY | BXBX |
| **Geographic / network-scale** | **MHYC** | **MESH** |

MHYC sits in the lower-left cell — the research practice for geographic and network-scale questions. **Its build-practice sibling is MESH.** Together they cover the geographic dimension of the studio's catalogue completely (research + build).

## § 5 · The strategic position

*(Mostly unchanged from v0.1.)*

MHYC does several things for the broader studio that are worth naming explicitly:

### 5.1 It establishes editorial authority before commercial pitch

A studio that publishes serious neighborhood research demonstrates the kind of judgment that customers eventually rely on for build decisions (BXBX dwellings, MESH tools). A reader spending months in MHYC's editorial corpus before encountering any studio product has been gradually persuaded of the studio's perspective without being sold to. Same architecture as 37signals / Stratechery / Tufte: research underwrites product.

### 5.2 It positions the studio against an honest market reality

Most travel-and-lifestyle content in 2026 operates in identity-aspirational register — "live anywhere," "be free," etc. MHYC operates in analytical register. *"Neighborhoods are the actual unit. Most of the world has known this forever. Here's how they relate. Here's what's currently interesting. Here's the math."* That positioning is unusual and defensible.

### 5.3 It opens consulting work

A studio with serious published research on the global hood-network becomes a credible consulting partner for:
- Cities developing tourism strategies for specific neighborhoods
- Real-estate developers exploring nomad-friendly neighborhoods
- Coworking and co-living operators sizing markets
- Other studios looking for cultural-analytical collaboration

### 5.4 It future-proofs the practice

The studio's bet on neighborhoods-as-the-actual-unit-of-meaning is robust regardless of how digital nomadism evolves. Even if the digital nomad phenomenon plateaus or recedes, **the underlying observation about neighborhood granularity remains valid**, and MHYC's research has documentary value.

## § 7 · Operating questions worth flagging

*(Refined from v0.1 — the matching-tool-specific questions move to MESH-thread; the editorial-and-research questions stay here.)*

**Q1 · Domain and publishing platform.** Working assumption: mhyc.m1nd.co as the subdomain. Publishing platform: Ghost (paralleling t1ny.m1nd.co). The matching tool lives at mhyc.m1nd.co/match but is technically a MESH product running on its own stack (Mapbox + Svelte per MESH-thread v0.1) and integrating with MHYC's Ghost content.

**Q2 · Profile cadence.** 2-3 profiles per quarter for year one, scaling to 4-5 per quarter once the format is mature. Stays here.

**Q3 · Contributor recruitment and compensation.** Some of MHYC's strongest work will come from people who genuinely know specific neighborhoods better than Jordan can. Pay per profile from the start, even at modest rates ($500-1,500 industry standard for that length). Establishes the studio as a serious editorial publisher, not a content farm.

**Q4 · Geographic prioritization.** First 10 profiles include both "expected" neighborhoods (LES, Brooklyn, Berlin, Lisbon) and at least 3-4 less-expected (Taipei, Mexico City peripheries, Bangkok, smaller European cities). Signal that MHYC isn't a "best digital nomad cities" listicle.

**Q5 · Trinity Bellwoods as the inaugural profile.** Yes — write what you know best, establish the editorial voice and format on home ground. The studio's authority partly comes from being a credible inhabitant of one of its profiled neighborhoods.

**Q6 · MHYC.0001 timing.** Launch profiles first; MHYC.0001 emerges as year-one synthesis. The studio can publish 5-10 profiles before MHYC.0001 needs to exist; the founding essay then reads as the studio's reflection on what the first year of practice revealed.

**Q7 · The community-curated matching question.** When does the matching tool open to community contributions? This is now a MESH operational question — see MESH-thread v0.1. MHYC's role in the answer is editorial review of submitted matches.
