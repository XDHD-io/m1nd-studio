## § 1 · What T1NY is

**T1NY is the studio's continuous research, reporting, and editorial practice covering the broader market for tiny, modular, conversion, and micro-dwelling solutions, with a global frame and a Toronto vantage point.** The publishing surface (probably *t1ny.m1nd.co*) produces:

- Regular market scans of tiny-home, van, school-bus, container, ADU, and conversion listings on Marketplace and elsewhere
- Comp analyses pricing the studio's own BXBX dwellings against the broader available market
- Field reports on urban-stealth deployments, dwelling-in-place strategies, and creative occupancy patterns
- Case studies of specific conversions (real builds the studio finds noteworthy)
- Global synthesis essays drawing on dwelling patterns from cities and countries where small-scale living is the historical norm
- A consulting-grade research format mirroring MEAL.0001 — written for a sophisticated reader who wants real analysis, not lifestyle content

T1NY is consciously **research-first**. The studio's tiny-dwelling work — the BXBX line including the Hako flagship, the Yado-S, the greenhouse and shed variants — gets *contextualized* within T1NY's market scans and global syntheses, not the other way around. **The studio is a participant in the tiny-dwelling market AND a serious analyst of it.** Both roles support each other.

### What T1NY is NOT

- **Not a manufacturing brand.** The studio doesn't sell "T1NY homes." BXBX makes specific dwellings; T1NY reports on the category.
- **Not a tiny-house-movement adjacent project.** The genre conventions of that movement (HGTV aesthetics, off-grid maximalism, anti-system politics, hippie-craft register) are not T1NY's register. T1NY's register is editorial-research — closer to *The Architectural Review*, *Dezeen*, or *Failed Architecture* than to *Tiny House Hunters*.
- **Not Toronto-myopic.** The thesis emerges from the Toronto vantage point but the research extends globally. Most of T1NY's strongest material will come from the studio's observations of cities and cultures where small-scale dwelling has been normal for decades or centuries.
- **Not a startup or VC-backed venture.** T1NY is a studio publishing practice. Its primary economic engine is the credibility and traffic it generates for the studio's other work — including BXBX sales and eventual consulting engagements — not direct subscription or media revenue.

## § 2 · The founding thesis · the Anglosphere is the historical outlier

This is the section that needed the biggest revision from v0.1. The original "Toronto Housing Failure" frame was correct as far as it went but too narrow to anchor the practice. **The corrected frame:**

**Most of the world lives in 200-400 square feet, has done so for decades or centuries, and is doing fine. The Anglosphere mythology of the 2,000 sq ft detached home with a garage and a lawn is a 20th-century historical anomaly, not a global norm. Toronto's affordability crisis is forcing North Americans to confront what billions of people elsewhere have always known — and to study the technologies, infrastructures, and design patterns that make small-scale dwelling actually work at population scale.**

Three pieces of evidence anchor this thesis:

**The historical evidence.** The single-family detached suburban home as the dominant housing type emerged in the United States and Canada specifically in the post-WWII period — roughly 1945-1980 — driven by GI Bill mortgages, federal highway construction, racially-restrictive zoning, white-flight urban planning, and cheap fossil fuels. It is approximately 75 years old as a dominant pattern, against millennia of human history of dense urban dwelling. **The exception masquerades as the norm.** Anyone treating the suburban detached home as the natural baseline is treating a brief, regionally-specific experiment as universal truth.

**The geographic evidence.** As of 2026, the median dwelling size in Japan is ~95 sq m / ~1,020 sq ft (and Tokyo dwellings frequently smaller). Hong Kong's nano-flats run 130-300 sq ft. Singapore's HDB units average 700-1,200 sq ft. European apartments in old urban cores routinely run 30-50 sq m / 320-540 sq ft. Bangkok and Saigon and Manila and Mumbai house tens of millions of people in dwellings far smaller than the Canadian average. **These are not failures of those cultures to achieve the suburban dream. They are functioning, often happy, accommodations to dense urban reality** — and they're supported by infrastructure, design patterns, and cultural habits the Anglosphere has been slow to study.

**The economic evidence.** The conventional Anglosphere path (rent → save → buy a detached starter home → trade up) has functionally closed for most working people in Toronto, Vancouver, Sydney, Auckland, much of the US west coast, London, and increasingly secondary cities. The data is unambiguous: in Toronto specifically, less than 7% of households can qualify for an average detached home at average prices, real rents have roughly doubled in 15 years while real wages tracked inflation, and the structural drivers (immigration policy, foreign capital flows, construction costs, zoning rigidity, political incentives favoring existing owners) are not reversing. **The traditional path is closed and is not reopening.**

Combine the three: **the path that Anglosphere culture treats as default is a recent historical experiment that has now functionally ended for most working people in the markets where the studio operates.** Pretending otherwise — continuing to behave as if traditional real estate is a viable path for the median Toronto worker — is increasingly an act of denial.

### What this opens

If the conventional path is closed AND the rest of the world has long demonstrated that small-scale dwelling works, the questions become:

- What can be learned from the cultures and cities that already solved this?
- What infrastructure (battery-swap stations, capsule modularity, mobile vendor systems, public transit density, micro-retail patterns) supports small-scale dwelling at scale?
- What design patterns (Japanese kyosho jutaku, Hong Kong nano-flats, Thai canal houses, Vietnamese tube houses, Mediterranean courtyards) actually work?
- What lineage of Western writers, travelers, and builders has been quietly demonstrating these patterns for decades before they became mainstream conversation? (Tynan, the original van-conversion crowd, the sailing-residence community, the trailer-as-permanent-home crowd, the urban-stealth and dwelling-in-place practitioners.)
- And then, locally: what does honest dwelling acquisition look like in Toronto specifically, drawing on this global toolkit?

T1NY's research practice operates in this question-space. **Every report assumes the reader is someone for whom the conventional Anglosphere path has failed or feels suspect, and is exploring alternatives seriously rather than aspirationally.**

The alternatives map roughly to:

- **Conversions:** vans, school buses, RVs, sailboats — vehicles converted into dwellings (the Western tradition)
- **Tiny structures on owned land:** ADUs, tiny homes on trailers, modular cabins on family/friends' property
- **Stealth urban dwelling:** living patterns that exploit zoning grey areas — parking-pad sleepers, garden offices used as dwellings, "office" lofts inhabited continuously
- **Cooperative arrangements:** land trusts, intentional communities, multi-family dwelling pooling
- **Geographic relocation:** leaving Toronto for markets where conventional purchasing remains feasible — small-city Ontario, the Maritimes, parts of the Prairies, rural America
- **Hybrid arrangements:** weekly remote-work paired with affordable rural housing; sailing-based partial-year dwellings
- **The imported patterns:** Japanese-style small dwelling, Hong Kong-style nano-flat efficiency, Thai mobility infrastructure adapted to Western context

T1NY covers all of these. **The studio's BXBX dwellings — Hako, Yado-S, etc. — are one specific answer in this much larger space of alternatives.** The strategic move is to position BXBX honestly: not as "the tiny home you should buy" but as "one well-designed answer among many real options worth understanding, drawn from a long lineage of global precedent."

## § 3 · The publishing format and rhythm

T1NY publishes on its own surface, separate from m1nd.co's editorial and stock.m1nd.co's food catalogue. Working subdomain: **t1ny.m1nd.co.**

### 3.1 The four inaugural research products

**Product 1 · The Quarterly Market Scan**
A ~5,000-word quarterly report covering the state of the alternative-dwelling market. Pulls 50-100 comps from Toronto Marketplace, kijiji, Craigslist, and specialized listings. Categorizes by type (van conversion, tiny on trailer, container conversion, ADU, etc.). Tracks pricing, build quality, geographic distribution, common features. **Includes commentary** — what's good, what's overpriced, what trends are emerging, what's getting harder to find.

Format: long-form article with embedded photographs, price/feature comparison tables, geographic distribution maps. Modeled on the kind of report a real-estate analyst would produce for institutional clients, applied to a non-traditional category.

**Product 2 · The Conversion Case Study**
Monthly. A deep dive on one specific conversion — a van, a school bus, a container, an ADU, a tiny on trailer. Photographs of the build (with permission). Interview with the builder/occupant. Analysis of what worked, what didn't, what it cost, what it would cost to replicate today. Honest about failures and shortcomings, not just success-porn.

Format: longform with interview Q&A, photo essay, BOM breakdown if obtainable.

**Product 3 · The Urban Stealth Field Report**
Quarterly or as-warranted. Documents specific patterns of stealth urban dwelling that the studio observes in Toronto or other major cities. This is the genuinely sensitive product — it requires careful anonymization, avoids enabling enforcement, and approaches the topic ethnographically rather than journalistically.

Worth being honest: stealth dwelling exists because zoning and economics have failed to provide affordable legal options. T1NY reports on the phenomenon as a real adaptation to a real failure; it does not glamourize illegal occupancy nor expose specific people. Most useful when treating patterns and adaptations rather than naming specific locations or people.

Format: ethnographic essay, anonymous case studies, photographs only of structures or situations (never identifiable people).

**Product 4 · The Studio Offerings, in Context**
Quarterly or per-release. When the studio releases a new BXBX dwelling — the Hako, the Yado-S, future variants — T1NY publishes a contextual analysis: how this offering compares to what's currently available on the market, what tradeoffs it represents, what kind of buyer it suits. **Genuinely honest about whether the studio's offering is competitive on price, on quality, on use case.** Not a sales pitch dressed as analysis.

Format: same depth as Product 2 (case study format) but covering the studio's own products. Cross-references the Quarterly Market Scan to show where the studio's offerings sit in the broader landscape.

### 3.2 Publishing rhythm

| Product | Frequency | Effort per piece |
|---|---|---|
| Quarterly Market Scan | 4×/year | ~40 hours research + writing |
| Conversion Case Study | 12×/year | ~15 hours per piece |
| Urban Stealth Field Report | 2-4×/year | ~25 hours per piece |
| Studio Offerings In Context | ~4-6×/year | ~20 hours per piece |

**Total annual editorial load: ~360-440 hours.** That's roughly one full month of studio work spread across the year. Heavy but feasible alongside the studio's other practices.

### 3.3 The publishing register

T1NY borrows from existing precedent in research-grade publishing:

- **Sentence-level register:** like *The Architectural Review* — precise, opinionated, knowledgeable, occasionally dryly funny
- **Visual register:** like *Dezeen* or *Apollo Magazine* — high-quality photography, restrained typography, generous whitespace
- **Analytical register:** like a McKinsey property-market report — comps, tables, charts, executive summaries
- **Critical register:** like *Failed Architecture* — willing to call out bullshit, willing to praise excellence, willing to take positions

What T1NY does NOT borrow from:
- Lifestyle blogs (genre conventions of personal narrative + sponsored content)
- Real-estate listing aggregators (just listings, no analysis)
- YouTube vanlife channels (entertainment register, not research register)
- HGTV tiny-house programming (drama-first format, low informational content)

## § 4 · Relationship to BXBX · the research-vs-build distinction

This is the architectural question that needs answering clearly.

**BXBX is the studio's build practice for tiny dwellings.** It produces specific physical dwellings — the Hako flagship (BXBX-005), the Yado-S (BXBX-006), the greenhouse variants (BXBX-016, BXBX-017), and future variants. Each has its own spec, its own BOM, its own construction process. BXBX outputs are *buildable objects*.

**T1NY is the studio's research practice for tiny dwellings.** It produces *editorial artifacts* — market scans, case studies, field reports, contextual analyses, global syntheses. T1NY outputs are *written and visual documents*.

The two practices interact deliberately:

- **BXBX informs T1NY:** Each new BXBX dwelling becomes a Product 4 piece in T1NY's catalogue (Studio Offerings In Context).
- **T1NY informs BXBX:** Each Quarterly Market Scan reveals what's missing in the market, what's overpriced, what use cases lack good solutions — informing BXBX's design priorities for future dwellings. Global syntheses also point BXBX toward design patterns proven elsewhere but missing locally.
- **T1NY contextualizes BXBX:** Customers shopping BXBX dwellings encounter T1NY first as research, then BXBX as one specific answer in the landscape that research describes. The customer is better-informed about the market when they buy. **The studio's product looks stronger when seen against the comps and the global precedents.**
- **BXBX's credibility comes partly from T1NY:** A studio that publishes serious quarterly market research and global synthesis essays is more credible as a dwelling designer than a studio that just sells dwellings. **Research practice underwrites build practice.**

### How customer-facing materials handle the distinction

Public catalogue (m1nd.co main page) lists practices clearly:
- **BXBX** — dwellings (with Hako, Yado-S, etc. as products)
- **T1NY** — research (with T1NY.0001, T1NY.0002, etc. as published reports)
- **KEEP / STOCK / GROW / SPORE / BDRC** — other practices

The customer interested in tiny dwellings encounters T1NY first (because the research is the public-facing front door), then is led through T1NY into BXBX as the studio's own dwelling offerings. The customer interested in BXBX directly can navigate there but encounters T1NY context inline (every BXBX product page links to its Studio Offerings In Context piece).

**Two practice surfaces, two distinct functions, one coherent customer journey.**

## § 5 · The strategic position (unchanged from v0.1)

T1NY does several things for the broader studio that are worth naming explicitly:

### 5.1 It establishes editorial authority before commercial pitch

Customers buying a $20-28K Hako need to trust the studio's design judgment before they commit. **A studio that publishes serious quarterly market research demonstrates that judgment continuously.** A customer reading T1NY for six months before encountering BXBX dwellings has been gradually persuaded of the studio's perspective — without ever being sold to.

This is the same architecture as 37signals' approach (write *Rework* and *Getting Real*, then customers come for the books and stay for Basecamp), Edward Tufte's approach (write *Visual Display of Quantitative Information*, then sell the workshops), or *The Browser* / *Stratechery* / similar editorial-credibility-first businesses. **Research underwrites product.**

### 5.2 It positions the studio against an honest market reality

Most tiny-home companies in 2026 operate in a fantasy register — selling "freedom from mortgage stress" or "minimalist living" or "off-grid independence" as identity products. **The studio operates in an analytical register.** *"The Anglosphere is the outlier. Most of the world has been doing this forever. Here's what the alternatives look like, here's the lineage, here's our specific answer in this landscape."* That positioning is unusual in this market and is genuinely defensible.

### 5.3 It opens consulting work

A studio with serious published research on the tiny-dwelling market becomes a credible consulting partner for:
- Municipalities planning ADU bylaws or microhousing pilots
- Real-estate developers exploring micro-unit projects
- Non-profits working on housing alternatives
- Other tiny-home builders looking for design or research collaboration

This consulting work pays meaningfully better than retail BXBX sales and is the studio's likely operating-revenue engine while the BXBX build practice scales. **T1NY's editorial output becomes the studio's consulting portfolio.**

### 5.4 It future-proofs the practice

The studio's bet on tiny dwellings is right if the housing crisis persists, and wrong if markets somehow normalize in the next decade. **The studio's bet on tiny-dwelling research is right either way** — even if the crisis abates, the research practice has documented the failure-and-adaptation moment, and that documentation has lasting value.

The build practice depends on a particular market continuing. The research practice depends only on the studio continuing to do good work. **T1NY is more robust than BXBX as a long-term studio engagement.**

## § 7 · The visual and editorial system (unchanged from v0.1)

T1NY needs its own visual identity within the M1ND.studio family — distinct from STOCK's hanko-and-archive-paper register and from BXBX's deck-and-design-doc register.

### Working visual notes

- **Palette:** Similar paper-and-ink foundation as the rest of the studio but with a cooler, more analytical color accent. Possibly a navy or charcoal in place of the vermillion hanko mark used for the food catalogue. **A research-grade palette, not a lifestyle palette.**
- **Typography:** Inherits the studio's Space Grotesk + Inter + IBM Plex Mono triad. Possibly adds a serif (Garamond or similar) for long-form essay register, matching the *Tao* and *Meditations* reprint work.
- **Layout:** More closely modeled on architectural-review publications than on lifestyle magazines. Photographs treated as documents (with captions, sources, dates) rather than as atmosphere.
- **The T1NY mark itself:** The wordmark needs design work. Working assumption: same number-as-letter idiom as M1ND and B1RN, with a treatment that signals research/analysis rather than lifestyle.

### Inaugural editorial standards

- Every comp pulled from a public listing gets archived (with original price, date, URL) so the analysis remains verifiable
- Every interview subject gets the option of attribution or pseudonym; pseudonyms used freely for stealth-dwelling subjects
- Photographs of structures: yes. Photographs of identifiable people: only with explicit consent.
- Pricing analysis distinguishes between asking price, sale price (if obtainable), and "actual cost to build/own" — three different numbers that often diverge significantly
- Geographic specificity: precise enough to be useful, vague enough to protect specific addresses when the subject requests it

## § 8 · Operating questions worth flagging

**Q1 · Domain decision.** Working assumption: t1ny.m1nd.co as a subdomain. Alternatives: t1ny.studio (its own root domain, $40/year), t1nyresearch.com, or just /t1ny path on m1nd.co. **My read:** subdomain on m1nd.co — keeps everything in the studio family, costs nothing additional, simpler operations. The brand benefits from being clearly part of M1ND.studio, not a separate entity.

**Q2 · Publishing platform.** T1NY needs to publish ~16-20 pieces per year, some quite long. WordPress is the default for long-form publishing; alternatives include Ghost (cleaner editorial focus, ~$10/month hosted), self-hosted on the same stack as m1nd.co (more work, more control), or Substack (best for email reach but compromises on design control). **My read:** Ghost hosted is the right tradeoff for studio register, design control, and operational simplicity. Substack later if email distribution becomes the primary growth vector.

**Q3 · Email and audience growth.** Does T1NY operate an email list from day one? Most editorial practices do; the alternative is purely web-published-and-shared. **My read:** yes from day one, opt-in via Ghost's built-in subscriber system. Quarterly cadence for the market scans gives subscribers a real reason to maintain the subscription. Don't push.

**Q4 · Author voice and attribution.** Is T1NY written under Jordan's name? Under "M1ND.studio"? Under a pen name? **My read:** under "M1ND.studio · T1NY" institutional attribution, with Jordan as identified principal in the about page. The institutional voice better matches the analytical register and allows for occasional guest contributors as the practice scales.

**Q5 · The Anglosphere-anomaly thesis · how directly to state it.** Section 2 of this document states the thesis bluntly. Public T1NY copy can be slightly more nuanced for the wider audience while keeping the analytical bite. **My read:** state it directly. The audience for T1NY is people who already know the conventional path is broken; soft-pedaling the thesis would just be performative caution.

**Q6 · Relationship to existing media.** Several Toronto-region publications cover housing seriously (*Spacing*, *The Local*, *blogTO* occasionally, *Toronto Life*, the various housing-policy newsletters). Should T1NY pursue cross-publication, op-ed placements, or republishing arrangements? **My read:** not in Phase 0-2. Build T1NY's own credibility first, then consider cross-publication in Phase 3+ once there's a back catalogue to license from.

**Q7 · The BXBX naming question, revisited.** Now that T1NY exists as a customer-facing umbrella for the research side, does BXBX stay as a product practice or does it merge into T1NY entirely? **My read:** BXBX stays. The studio's build practice deserves its own name and its own internal architecture. T1NY is the editorial frame; BXBX is the build practice. Customers encounter both; internally the studio operates both. Same architecture as STOCK (publishing/catalogue brand) vs BDRC (specific product practice within the food catalogue).

**Q8 · T1NY.0001's specific shape and title.** Pending the genesis-collection document. Once Jordan's inventory of inspirations is reasonably complete, T1NY.0001 can be structured properly. Working candidates for title and structure in § 6 are placeholders.

## § 9 · Closing

T1NY exists because the studio has been doing tiny-dwelling work for years and never had the right name for the **research and editorial layer** of that work. The Hako, the Yado-S, the BXBX series — these are *what the studio builds*. T1NY is *what the studio writes about, thinks about, and publishes regarding* the broader category in which those dwellings live.

The Toronto vantage point is real and matters — Jordan has lived this, observed the Anglosphere housing failure up close, and writes from a specific perspective. But the *content* of T1NY's research draws on a much larger global synthesis. **Most of the world has lived small for a very long time and is doing fine.** That's the inheritance T1NY's research connects North American readers to: the technologies, infrastructures, design patterns, and dwelling traditions that have always existed elsewhere and are now relevant locally because the conventional Anglosphere path has functionally closed.

T1NY.0001 — the founding statement of the practice — is bigger than any single research report the studio has produced. It needs its full inheritance properly catalogued before it can be written to publication standard. The companion document **T1NY.0001-genesis-collection.md** exists for exactly this purpose: a structured place for Jordan to capture the inspirations as they surface, over weeks if needed, before the synthesis essay gets drafted.

The work from here:

4. Begin Q4 2026 Market Scan research immediately so T1NY.0002 ships in winter cycle
