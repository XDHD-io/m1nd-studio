# KEEP · 1646 cell modifications · thread v0.1

## A case study on what the 1646 cell becomes when you accessorize it properly.

**Date:** 2026-05-19
**Status:** Practice extension · documentation of mod patterns
**Codename:** 1646-mods (working name for the mod taxonomy; reinforced variant proposed as **1646-R**)
**Parent:** KEEP
**Companion documents:** KEEP-thread-v0.4 (1646 cell standard), CODEX-thread-v0.1 (card-based publishing)
**This document:** four mod categories, each pulling the cell in a different direction, and the architectural question of what 1646 becomes once it's modded

---

## § 0 · The question this document answers

The 1646 cell, as established in KEEP-thread-v0.4, is a commodity envelope — a 4×6 photo storage case with a snap-tight latch and clear plastic body, sourced from any photo-supply vendor. The studio's contribution wasn't inventing it; it was *naming* it (the 4-inch and 6-inch standard interior dimensions give 1646 its name) and adopting it as the universal storage unit across every practice in the catalogue.

But several questions have been quietly accumulating about what the cell can become with cheap accessories and small modifications:

1. What does **bulk sourcing at scale** do to the cost structure of every system that uses 1646?
2. Can the cell be **subdivided internally** without losing its commodity-envelope identity?
3. Can the cell be **transport-rated** — taken outside the Wowlive shelf — and stay secured?
4. Can the cell be **made more durable** without losing its "anyone can replace it" sourcing story?

This thread documents the four mod categories that answer those four questions, with the architectural framing that **each mod pulls 1646 along a different vector** rather than just adding accessories to a single object.

---

## § 1 · The four mod vectors

| Vector | Mod | What it changes about the cell |
|---|---|---|
| **Sourcing** | Bulk procurement (Novelinks 24-pack) | The cell's cost. Doesn't change what it is. |
| **Subdivision** | 4×6 ziplock bags inside | The cell's interior. From single chamber to organizable. |
| **Mobility** | Rubber band straps | The cell's portability. From static archive to transport-rated. |
| **Durability** | Paint, coating, latch-reinforcement | The cell's quality grade. From commodity to studio-spec'd. |

The vectors compose. A 1646 cell that's been bulk-sourced, ziplock-subdivided, rubber-banded for transport, AND coating-reinforced is doing all four things at once — and at that point, **it's not really the commodity envelope anymore.** It's something else. That something else needs a name; see §6 below.

## § 2 · Sourcing · the Novelinks 24-pack discovery

### 2.1 · The specs

- **Product:** Novelinks Photo Case 24-Pack (Amazon B07PHWGFYP)
- **Price:** $29.99 CAD
- **Per-cell cost:** $1.25
- **Single case dimensions:** 4.75 × 6.70 × 1.23 inches (matches the 1646 internal spec exactly)
- **Capacity per cell:** 100 photos / cards (review-confirmed at 46–70 in practice, depending on what's inside)
- **Closure:** snap-tight latch on each individual case
- **Body:** transparent rigid plastic, label slot on each case
- **Outer carrier:** 12.8 × 15.74 × 5.43 inches with handle, organizes the 24 inner cases
- **Origin:** Made in USA

### 2.2 · What this does to the studio's economics

At the prior assumption of ~$2.50 per cell (small-pack pricing), the 1646 system carried a real material cost that mattered at scale. The Novelinks 24-pack changes the math:

| Configuration | Old cost ($2.50/cell) | New cost ($1.25/cell) | Saved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single Hako KEEP wall (~84 cells) | $210 | $105 | **$105 · 50%** |
| STOCK half-wall starter (~40 cells) | $100 | $50 | **$50** |
| CODEX library of 100 titles | $250 | $125 | **$125** |
| BDRC + COOK kitchen build (~50 cells) | $125 | $63 | **$62** |

**Across every configuration, materials cost drops by 50%.** This is not a small win. It does three things at once:

1. **A full Hako KEEP wall is now under $110 in cells.** The cell-cost objection to going all-in on 1646 mostly disappears.
2. **The studio can give cells away in certain contexts** (e.g., one cell with every CODEX purchase, or a starter cell with every STOCK product) without it being a meaningful margin hit.
3. **CODEX-001 and the Studio Classics Series become more viable** — a customer buying a CODEX no longer has to think about whether they want to invest in a cell for it. At $1.25, the cell is a rounding error.

### 2.3 · The honest caveats

Some reviews note that the Novelinks plastic is "thin" and the latches "feel like they could snap off with heavy use." This is the durability question, addressed in §5. **The 24-pack is the right base spec, not the final form.** A studio that sells CODEX titles or STOCK products in 1646 cells should likely ship the *base* cell at the Novelinks spec and offer reinforcement mods as a premium upgrade.

The carrier case itself (the 12.8" × 15.74" × 5.43" outer box) is also worth noting — it's essentially a transport crate for 24 cells, with a handle. This becomes relevant for §4 (mobility) below.

### 2.4 · Sourcing recommendation

**Adopt the Novelinks 24-pack as the canonical base-spec source for 1646.** Keep the smaller packs (10-pack, 16-pack) for customers who only need a few. Update KEEP-thread to reflect $1.25 per cell as the canonical bulk cost. Update every downstream practice (STOCK, BDRC, COOK, CODEX) that quotes per-cell cost.

## § 3 · Subdivision · the ziplock-bag interior pattern

### 3.1 · The accessory

- **Product:** 4×6 reclosable poly bags, 2 mil, 100-pack (Amazon B07C4TBB76 and similar)
- **Per-bag cost:** ~$0.02-0.15 depending on vendor and quantity (Jordan's noted price of ~$0.02 is achievable in bulk-vendor 1000-packs)
- **Material:** clear low-density polyethylene, FDA/USDA food-safe, BPA free
- **Usable dimensions:** 4"W × 6"H plus 0.1-0.15" extra for fit
- **Closure:** zipper top, reclosable, heat-sealable above the zipline if tamper-evidence is needed
- **Industry parlance:** "apple baggies," "dispensary bags," "parts bags" depending on context

### 3.2 · What ziplock bags buy in the 1646 ecosystem

The 1646 cell, as a single chamber, is great for *one kind of thing in volume* — 100 cards of a CODEX, a stack of BDRC recipe cards, a spice mix in a sealed container. It's less great for **mixed-component contents** where the customer needs to access one component without disturbing the others.

Ziplock bags solve this by creating **modular sub-units inside the cell.** Six concrete patterns:

#### Pattern A · Component subdivision (BDRC, COOK)
A complex BDRC cell containing multiple spice components, each in its own ziplock bag inside the cell. The customer pulls the cinnamon bag without disturbing the cardamom or the star anise. The cell becomes a *kit* rather than a *container*.

#### Pattern B · Moisture protection (SPORE, GROW, MEAL)
Grain spawn for SPORE mushroom kits, seed packets for GROW, dried herbs for COOK — all materials where the customer wants the cell open to label and inspection but the contents sealed against humidity. The ziplock provides a second moisture envelope inside the cell.

#### Pattern C · Tamper-evident sealing (commercial STOCK shipments)
For STOCK products that ship to customers, the heat-sealable variant of the ziplock above its zipper creates a tamper-evident inner package. The customer can see the cell hasn't been opened in transit. After delivery the customer cuts the heat seal and the bag works as a normal reclosable bag for the life of the product.

#### Pattern D · Liquid containment (oils, tinctures)
Cells normally store dry goods. With a ziplock as the inner container, the cell can hold a small bottle of oil, tincture, or extract — the ziplock catches any leakage and isolates spills from the rest of the cell. This unlocks several COOK and SPORE products that otherwise would have required different storage.

#### Pattern E · Quick-grab modular units (travel, vehicle, festival contexts)
The reader/customer pulls out one or two ziplock bags from a cell, drops them in a backpack or daypack, and the rest of the cell stays home. The ziplock is the *unit of portability*; the cell is the *unit of archive*. This is structurally important — it separates "where the thing lives" from "how the thing travels."

#### Pattern F · The reader's own organization (CODEX, KEEP archive)
A CODEX cell has 100 cards including front matter, body, and reflection cards. A reader who's read 20 chapters might want to keep those 20 cards separate from the unread cards. A ziplock bag inside the cell labeled "read · chapters 1-20" enables this without disturbing the original card sequence.

### 3.3 · What ziplock bags do NOT do

They don't add structural strength to the cell, they don't provide UV protection, they don't help with stacking. They are exclusively about **interior organization and moisture isolation.** Treat them as a subdivision mod, not a durability mod.

### 3.4 · Sourcing recommendation

**Add 4×6 ziplock bags to the KEEP standard kit as an optional accessory.** Stock both 100-pack consumer-grade and 1000-pack wholesale for shop-level use. Document the six patterns above in the KEEP customer documentation. **Especially flag Pattern E** (quick-grab modular travel units) since it's the architectural one — it's not just a different way to use a cell, it's a different concept of what the cell is for.

## § 4 · Mobility · the rubber band transport mod

### 4.1 · The problem

A 1646 cell sitting on a Wowlive shelf in a quiet apartment is fine. A 1646 cell going somewhere — into a backpack, into a car trunk, into checked luggage, into a kitchen prep station, into a workshop where things get bumped — has a failure mode the snap-tight latch alone doesn't fully prevent. **The latch holds the lid shut against gravity and gentle force, but a sharp impact or sustained pressure on the wrong axis can pop it.** Reviews confirm this concern with the Novelinks plastic specifically.

### 4.2 · The mod

A simple rubber band looped around the cell — circumferentially, or in a cross-strap pattern over the top — adds a second layer of closure-security that's faster to apply and cheaper than any other approach.

**Sourcing:** standard #64 rubber bands (3.5" × 1/4") at ~$5 per 100 in bulk packs. The #64 size fits a 1646 cell circumferentially with appropriate tension. #84 (3.5" × 1/2") are heavier-duty for shipping/transport contexts. Both should be in the studio's mod kit.

### 4.3 · Three strap patterns, three contexts

#### Pattern 1 · Single circumferential (long axis)
One band around the 6.7" axis of the cell. Holds the latch closed against any axis except sliding along the band's circumference. **Use for:** short transport (in a bag, in a car, around the house), routine shelf-to-counter movement.

#### Pattern 2 · Cross-strap (X-pattern)
Two bands, one on each axis, crossing at the top. Holds the latch closed against all impact directions. **Use for:** longer transport (checked luggage, festival travel, shipping), cells that get jostled hard. This is the recommended pattern for any cell leaving the home.

#### Pattern 3 · Stack-strap (multiple cells)
Multiple cells stacked together with bands wrapping the entire stack. **Use for:** moving a STOCK shipment, transporting an entire CODEX library subset (e.g., taking 5 CODEX titles to a cabin for a weekend), or moving the contents of one Wowlive shelf to another location.

### 4.4 · The architectural shift

**Rubber bands are tiny, but they change what 1646 is.** Without them, the 1646 cell is implicitly *static* — it lives on a shelf and rarely leaves it. With them, the cell is *transport-rated* — it can leave the shelf and go somewhere with confidence. This unlocks:

- **Festival/travel CODEX:** Take 3 CODEX titles in a daypack for a weekend trip. Bands keep the cells closed; the reader gets a portable library.
- **Mobile STOCK kitchens:** A chef takes a BDRC cell + a COOK cell + a SPORE cell to a pop-up event. Bands make this safe.
- **Shipping CODEX/STOCK products:** A shipped cell with bands applied is essentially as protected as a sealed product.
- **Workshop/site bags:** A repair professional's parts cells can be banded and tossed in a tool bag without contents scattering.

The rubber band turns out to be the cheapest single mod that most affects what the system can do.

### 4.5 · Caveats

Rubber bands degrade over time, especially with UV exposure or extreme temperatures. They are *not* a long-term archival closure — for cells that stay on a shelf for years, the latch alone is correct and adding a band would just create rubber residue or breakage. **Bands are for transport, not for archive.** Apply when moving, remove when shelving.

## § 5 · Durability · the case-reinforcement mods

### 5.1 · The honest assessment of the base case

Reviews of the Novelinks photo cases consistently note two things: the plastic is "thin," and the latches "feel like they could snap off with heavy use." This isn't a critique of Novelinks specifically; it's the trade-off built into the $1.25-per-cell price point. **A cell at $1.25 is going to be lighter-duty than a cell at $5 or $10.** That's fine — but it means the studio should be honest about which use cases need reinforcement and which don't.

### 5.2 · Mod ladder · five options from cheapest to most involved

#### Mod 1 · Hot glue latch reinforcement (~$0.05 per cell, ~30 seconds)
A small dab of hot glue inside the latch mechanism firms up the click and prevents the latch from working loose with repeated opening/closing. The most-recommended mod from the reviews themselves. **Use for:** any cell that gets opened and closed frequently (kitchen BDRC cells, active workshop cells, daily-use CODEX cells).

#### Mod 2 · Hinge reinforcement (~$0.10 per cell, ~2 minutes)
A tiny zip-tie or piece of wire through the hinge axis adds reinforcement against the hinge failing under stress. Less commonly needed than latch reinforcement; reserve for cells that take real hinge stress (e.g., cells stored open as kitchen prep mise-en-place).

#### Mod 3 · Plasti Dip / rubberized coating (~$0.50-1.00 per cell, ~10 minutes per cell + 4-hour cure)
A spray-can rubberized coating applied to the cell's exterior. Adds significant impact resistance, hides scratches, can be applied in studio colors (ink black, hanko vermillion, paper cream, slate gray, etc.). A single $10-12 can covers approximately 10-20 cells. **Use for:** the premium reinforcement tier — STOCK products that ship, CODEX titles that should feel substantial, professional kitchen cells that take abuse.

#### Mod 4 · Edge banding with fabric tape (~$0.25-0.50 per cell, ~5 minutes)
A thin strip of book-binding tape or similar around the edges of the lid where it meets the body. Protects against edge wear and creates a more refined visual line. **Use for:** display cells, gift-grade CODEX presentations, cells whose visual quality matters more than impact resistance.

#### Mod 5 · Studio-branded vinyl wrap (~$2-4 per cell, ~15 minutes)
A custom-cut vinyl wrap applied to the exterior, in the studio's visual register (paper-and-hanko palette, Space Grotesk typography, studio mark). This is the *highest-tier mod* and turns the cell from "modified commodity" into "fully studio-branded object." **Use for:** flagship deliverables, customer gifting, retail/display contexts.

### 5.3 · The mod ladder as price-tiering

The five mods above also work as **product tiers** for the studio:

| Tier | Mods applied | Per-cell cost | Use case |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Base** | None — raw Novelinks | $1.25 | Default catalogue offering |
| **Working** | Latch + hinge reinforcement | $1.40 | Daily-use cells (kitchen, workshop) |
| **Premium** | + Plasti Dip coating | $2.00-2.50 | STOCK products, premium CODEX |
| **Display** | + Edge banding | $2.50-3.00 | Gift presentations, display cells |
| **Studio** | + Vinyl wrap | $5-7 | Flagship deliverables, retail |

**Even at the top tier the cell is still under $7.** The studio can offer five different quality levels of the same fundamental object and never exceed the cost of a single mass-market hardcover book.

### 5.4 · What durability mods do NOT solve

They don't make the cell waterproof (a flood ruins it regardless of how it's coated). They don't make it crush-proof (a heavy box dropped on it still wins). They don't extend the *archival* lifespan past what the plastic itself can support — for true archival storage (decades), the cell should still live on a shelf out of direct light. **The mods are about handling and use; not about archival permanence.**

## § 6 · The naming question · base-spec versus modded-spec

If the studio is shipping cells at five different tiers (Base, Working, Premium, Display, Studio), it needs a way to *talk about* the difference. A few options:

### Option A · 1646-R (Reinforced)
Single suffix indicating any modded variant. Simple, easy to reference. Loses fidelity (a Plasti-Dipped cell and a vinyl-wrapped cell are both "1646-R"). **Pro:** clean nomenclature. **Con:** doesn't disambiguate.

### Option B · 1646-R0 through 1646-R4
Numeric tier system. 1646-R0 = base, 1646-R1 = latch/hinge, 1646-R2 = coated, 1646-R3 = edge-banded, 1646-R4 = vinyl-wrapped. **Pro:** precise. **Con:** clunky to say.

### Option C · Tier names without suffix
"1646 Base," "1646 Working," "1646 Premium," "1646 Display," "1646 Studio." More marketing-friendly, less engineering-precise. **Pro:** speaks to customers directly. **Con:** loses the "1646" as a single canonical identifier.

### Option D · 1646 remains the cell, mods are separate SKUs
The cell is always just "1646." The mods are sold separately as "1646 mod kits" (latch reinforcement kit, coating kit, etc.). The customer assembles their own tier. **Pro:** maintains 1646 as a single object. **Con:** less polished as a customer-facing offering.

**Studio recommendation:** start with **Option D** (1646 stays canonical, mods are separate SKUs) for the catalogue's first year, then graduate to **Option B** (numeric tier) once enough customers are buying enough finished cells that a single-SKU tier system becomes operationally easier. The numeric tier system also future-proofs against new mod categories.

## § 8 · The architectural payoff · what 1646 actually is now

Coming back to the question that opened this document: **what is the 1646 cell once it's been modded?**

Before mods, 1646 is *the commodity envelope the studio standardized on.* Anyone can replace it; the studio's only contribution is the naming and the spec.

After mods, 1646 is *the studio's standardized cell platform.* The base cell is still a commodity, but the system around it — the sourcing economics, the subdivision patterns, the transport rating, the durability ladder, the visual register — is the studio's own work and not replaceable by going to any photo-supply store.

This is an important shift. KEEP's value proposition becomes less about "we picked a good box" and more about **"we operate the entire ecosystem around a good box."** The cell is the substrate; KEEP is the operating system.

CODEX, STOCK, BDRC, COOK, SPORE, GROW all sit on top of this platform. **A customer who buys into the 1646 system is buying into the platform, not just the box.**

## § 9 · Open questions

**Q1 · Do customers want pre-modded cells or DIY?** Buying a pre-Plasti-Dipped cell in studio colors is convenient; doing it yourself is more involved but cheaper and customizable. The studio likely needs to offer both. Which is the default catalogue offering?

**Q2 · Is there a sixth mod category?** This document covers sourcing, subdivision, mobility, and durability. Are there other vectors? Possibilities: **labeling** (insert cards, exterior labels — already partly a KEEP feature), **stacking systems** (custom risers or trays for multi-cell organization outside the Wowlive shelf), **security** (locks, tamper-evidence beyond heat-seal). These may want their own sections in a future thread version.

**Q3 · How do mod patterns interact with the Wowlive shelf?** A Plasti-Dipped cell is slightly thicker than a raw cell. Does it still fit cleanly in the Wowlive cell slots, or does the slot need adjustment? **Action:** measure with calipers once mods are applied to confirm fit. Edge banding adds maybe 1mm per side — likely fine. Vinyl wrap adds <0.5mm — fine. Plasti Dip can add 2-4mm depending on coats — needs verification.

**Q4 · What about the carrier case?** The Novelinks 24-pack includes an outer carrying box (12.8 × 15.74 × 5.43 inches with handle). Does the studio do anything with this, or is it discarded after unboxing? **Possibility:** the carrier becomes the *travel crate* for the Mobility vector — a "go bag" of 24 cells you can grab and take somewhere. Worth naming.

**Q5 · Is there a CODEX use case where the mods themselves get documented as a CODEX?** A "KEEP 1646 Mods" CODEX — 100 cards documenting every mod pattern, with how-to diagrams, parts lists, and customer worksheets. The studio's first *instructional* CODEX rather than a literary one. Possible Studio Classics Series companion title.

## § 10 · Closing

The four mod categories — sourcing, subdivision, mobility, durability — each pull 1646 along a different vector and *compose*. A cell that's been bulk-sourced from a 24-pack, subdivided with ziplock bags, banded for transport, and Plasti-Dipped for durability is no longer a commodity envelope. It is a studio-spec'd cell platform component.

The catalogue should reflect this. KEEP isn't just "the practice that named a box." It's **the practice that operates the entire ecosystem around the box** — the sourcing, the mods, the mod kit, the documentation, and the operational standards that connect 1646 to STOCK, BDRC, COOK, CODEX, SPORE, GROW, and every future practice that needs storage.

The Novelinks 24-pack at $1.25/cell makes this affordable. The ziplock subdivision makes it organizable. The rubber bands make it portable. The reinforcement ladder makes it durable. **Combined, these turn the 1646 cell from a box into a platform.** And a platform is what the studio actually needs.
