## § 0 · Why this exists

The studio has established the 1646 cell as the universal storage unit and KEEP as its operating platform. The studio has demonstrated the format works at $1.25/cell base-spec. **The question this document answers is: what should actually go in the cells?**

This is a brainstorm. Not every cell here should be productized; some are obviously stronger than others; some are flagged as "speculation" rather than "queued." The goal is a wide net so the studio can pick from a large pool rather than designing in a vacuum.

**Productization tiers** used throughout:
- 🟢 **FLAGSHIP** — ship within 6 months, central to the practice's identity
- 🟡 **QUEUED** — strong candidate, ship within 12 months once flagship is established
- 🔵 **CANDIDATE** — worth designing once nearby flagships exist
- ⚪ **SPECULATION** — interesting idea, may or may not survive the cull

**Cost notation:** "$X cell + $Y contents = $Z all-in COGS" then a target retail. Per-cell base assumed at $1.25 (Novelinks 24-pack).

## § 2 · MEAL · curated subscription kits (NEW · store category)

**This is the new model.** MEAL kits are *not* single-cell products. They are curated multi-cell bundles that draw from STOCK's existing SKU pool (COOK + BDRC + GROW + SPORE) and ship in Canada Post flat-rate boxes. The studio's value-add is the **curatorial judgment** — picking which cells go together and why.

Each MEAL kit is named for what it contains and ships in a specific CP box size. The "fill the box" motif is the design constraint that makes the math work.

## § 13 · Subscription economics · what the math says

Critical framing first: **the studio's MEAL kit is not HelloFresh.** It's an artisanal pantry subscription closer to Penzeys / RawSpice Bar / Atlas Obscura snack box in product type and price band.

| Service comparison | What ships | Monthly price |
|---|---|---|
| HelloFresh / Chefs Plate | Fresh perishables, complete meals | $160-240 |
| Universal Yums | International snack box | $14-39 |
| RawSpice Bar / Penzeys | Spice subscription | $9-25 |
| Atlas Obscura snack box | Curated pantry items | $40-55 |
| Trade Coffee | Coffee + tasting notes | $16-22 |
| **M1ND MEAL-S kit** | **4-cell pantry kit, shelf-stable** | **$64-89** |
| **M1ND MEAL-M kit** | **12-cell pantry quarterly** | **$149-199** |

The studio sits at the **premium end of the artisanal-pantry category** — higher than Penzeys (because the kits are more elaborate and include recipe cards / cross-practice content) but lower than HelloFresh (because nothing perishable ships).

**Margin model:**
- COGS = cells ($1.25 × N) + ingredients ($5-12 per cell average) + box ($22-33 postage) + studio overhead/labor ($10-25)
- Retail = COGS / (1 - margin); 28-35% margin target depending on tier
- The flat-rate-box constraint actually *helps* — shipping is a known fixed cost, not a variable per-pound calculation

**Subscription vs. à la carte:**
- À la carte at $24-32 for a single COOK or BDRC cell + $22 to ship = ~$50 total. **Works for premium gifting; not for casual purchase.**
- Subscriptions amortize the shipping cost across higher-margin recurring revenue. **The viable model.**

## § 14 · Open questions for the studio

1. **MEAL kits vs. individual STOCK cells — what's the relationship?** Does an MEAL kit subscriber also have access to à la carte? Are MEAL kit contents *exclusively* curated, or can customers cherry-pick from STOCK and have it shipped as a kit?

2. **Frequency cadence.** Monthly is the obvious default. Is there a quarterly option (CP Medium)? Annual (CP Large)? Weekly is probably too aggressive for shelf-stable products.

3. **Pause / skip flexibility.** Standard for subscription boxes. Implementation question for the cart UI.

4. **Sourcing scale.** Studio operating at hobbyist scale can hand-pack 10-50 boxes per month easily. Scaling to 200+ requires either co-packing partnerships or significant studio time. **The right ceiling for year 1 is probably 50-75 active subscribers.**

5. **Regional / international.** MEAL kits are designed against Canada Post flat-rate boxes. US shipping requires USPS Priority Mail flat-rate (similar logic, different prices). International is much harder due to customs on food ingredients. **Year 1: Canada only. Year 2: US. Beyond that: case-by-case.**

6. **Storefront UX.** Does the cart UI we built for the MVP support subscription billing? (Probably not out of the box — needs Stripe Billing or similar integration. **Implementation deferred; mockup is fine for year-1 catalogue.**)

7. **The "fill the box" motif you mentioned in the prompt.** I'm interpreting this as: every MEAL kit is designed to maximally fill its corresponding CP flat-rate box, which becomes a visual design constraint in the product photography and customer experience ("Open the box. The cells are packed in. There's no wasted space"). **Worth committing to as a visual signature.**
